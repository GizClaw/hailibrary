# Vocabulary source policy

Select sources by locale and claim type. Search current pages and open the actual entry or document. A search result snippet is not evidence.

## General hierarchy

1. Government, language regulator, standards body, or official curriculum document for normative and alignment claims.
2. An established monolingual learner's dictionary from a recognized lexicographic publisher for current pronunciation, sense, and part of speech.
3. A reputable general dictionary or specialist terminology database for corroboration.
4. Corpora, bilingual dictionaries, encyclopedias, and community sources only as supporting context.

When sources disagree, identify dialect, region, register, sense, date, and prescriptive versus descriptive purpose. Do not silently choose the first result.

## English (`en-US`)

Prefer current entries from:

- Oxford Learner's Dictionaries (`oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com`) for learner senses, part of speech, American IPA, and usage;
- Cambridge Dictionary (`dictionary.cambridge.org/us`) for US pronunciation, IPA, sense, and usage cross-checks;
- Merriam-Webster (`merriam-webster.com`) for American pronunciation and sense corroboration.

Use official curriculum/assessment publications for alignment, such as Cambridge English word lists when the entry declares a Cambridge alignment. Verify the exact edition, list, headword, and level; a dictionary entry does not prove curriculum placement.

If UK and US pronunciations differ, store the supported US form for `en-US` and mention the distinction in the review. Do not combine symbols from different transcription systems.

## Simplified Chinese (`zh-CN`)

Use Han Dian at `https://www.zdic.net/` to check modern word entries, character readings, pinyin, senses, and historical or variant information. The unavailable `zdict.cn` spelling should be interpreted as this Han Dian resource unless the user supplies a different site.

For normative conclusions, corroborate Han Dian with the applicable official source:

- Ministry of Education and State Language Commission materials (`moe.gov.cn`) for the Hanyu Pinyin Scheme, Putonghua pronunciation standards, language proficiency vocabulary, and language-use specifications;
- national standards published through government or the national standards system (`gov.cn`, `openstd.samr.gov.cn`) for script, orthography, romanization, and technical specifications;
- official terminology resources from the National Committee for Terms in Sciences and Technologies for specialist scientific or technical terms.

Use the current `通用规范汉字表` for simplified-character status, the current `普通话异读词审音表` or successor normative document for disputed readings, and the declared official vocabulary framework for level alignments. Confirm the exact word or entry number rather than inferring from a component character.

For pinyin:

- preserve lexical tone marks and standard syllable spelling;
- write neutral-tone syllables without tone marks;
- distinguish dictionary citation tone from contextual tone sandhi when relevant;
- verify polyphonic characters in the exact word and story sense;
- do not use Taiwan Mandarin pronunciation as proof for `zh-CN`.

## Additional locales

Prefer that locale's national language authority, official orthography/pronunciation standard, and recognized monolingual learner dictionary. Then use an official curriculum list for alignment. Document the chosen hierarchy in the review when the repository adds a locale not covered above.

Do not use a translation engine, model output, Wiktionary, or an unsourced word-list mirror as the sole authority. They may help locate a candidate term but cannot establish a normative or curriculum claim by themselves.
