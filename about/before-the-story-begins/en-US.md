# Before the Story Begins

**Isabella / 熊跳跳**

![People gather around firelight as their stories grow like seeds into a garden leading toward the wider world](artwork/story-garden.webp)

Long before writing existed, people gathered around firelight to tell stories.

Before we had books, we carried memory in the human voice. Before we had schools, stories taught us about seasons and distant places, about courage and love. Learning a language is not simply a matter of collecting more words. It is how we learn to describe the world before us, understand a life different from our own, and give a voice to something we feel inside.

Hai! Library begins here.

With the help of AI, we hope to plant an open garden of stories. It is not a factory for producing content, and we do not want every story to speak in the same voice. We care about something else: whether each language can keep its own breath, whether cultures can meet without setting down their memories, and whether every reader can follow a story a little way beyond the life they already know.

## Writers: The Eyes Through Which a Story Begins

![A compass on blank pages opens several paths toward different lives, observations, and imagined worlds](artwork/writers-compass.webp)

Many Writers live in this garden.

They are not copies of real authors, and they do not imitate anyone's recognizable voice. Each Writer is a name given to an original creative point of view. One may find mysteries on rain-washed streets; another may watch a city through the eyes of an animal. Some care about family, migration, and growing up. Others turn a scientific question into a gentle adventure.

The inspiration for our Writers comes from reading and from the particular ways people live around the world. We read those who came before us not to take their sentences, characters, or stories, but to understand something wider: what people value, how they meet the unknown, how they observe nature, and what kind of world they hope to leave behind.

These scattered inspirations are then remade as original personalities, interests, values, and creative choices. One Writer may be warm and curious, always finding wonder in an ordinary day. Another may be precise and patient, asking characters to change their minds when new evidence appears. Each has questions worth following and boundaries worth keeping: never borrowing a real author's voice, never taking protected characters or plots, and never treating culture as decoration.

A Writer is not a famous author hidden inside a machine. A Writer is a compass that helps creation choose a direction. It reminds us that a story should know more than what happened. It should also know why it looks from this place, and what it hopes to leave with the reader.

## Styles: The Shape in Which a World Appears

![One landscape passes through watercolor, crayon, torn paper, clay, and woodblock-like visual materials](artwork/styles-landscape.webp)

If Writers shape where we stand when looking at the world, Styles shape how that world appears.

A page may carry the warmth of crayon marks or the softness of watercolor spreading across paper. It may have the layers of cut-paper collage, the tactile charm of clay, the strength of a woodblock print, or the clear and lively rhythm of a comic. A Style is more than a new coat for an image. The temperature of its colors, the touch of its materials, the direction of its light, and the distance between a character and the reader all quietly change the way a story is felt.

These Styles are not substitutes for individual artists. They grow from materials, crafts, and visual languages that people have developed over generations. We describe medium, color, shape, composition, light, and texture without asking AI to reproduce a real person. Each Style should feel like a kind of weather: giving the story a distinct atmosphere while leaving room for the reader's imagination.

## One Story, Growing Again in Every Language

We do not want to carry a story word for word from one language into another.

True communication is not the replacement of words; it is the arrival of meaning. The same sentence may hold a different pause, warmth, or closeness in another language. A metaphor that shines in one culture may not illuminate the same place in another. Every Hai! Library edition therefore begins with the same story, characters, and pictures, but is written anew in the natural rhythm of its own language.

The editions are not shadows of one another. They are branches growing from the same seed. They preserve the same meaning and an equivalent learning challenge while speaking in voices that belong to their languages—like one melody played by different instruments.

## AI Is a Gardener, Not the Owner of the Garden

![People tend a garden of pages, plants, and connecting light beneath a sky full of distinct stars](artwork/ai-gardener.webp)

AI is neither a replacement for the author nor a teacher standing above the reader.

Here, it is closer to a patient gardener: helping people plant, arrange, and connect ideas, and making possible some creations that language, distance, or cost once prevented. It can help us explore new forms of expression, care for the learning difficulty of each language, and allow one wordless illustration to accompany readers in many different places.

But the purpose of the garden, the people it welcomes, what deserves to be planted, and how it should grow must always be guided by human judgment, responsibility, and care. We will check knowledge carefully, respect the boundaries of cultures and creators, and ask technology to practice restraint when restraint is needed.

Hai! Library is a public-interest project. We do not expect it to solve every problem in education and human communication at once. We simply want to do one small thing with care: help knowledge travel farther without losing its warmth; let languages meet without erasing their differences; and give more people the chance to read, speak, listen, and rediscover themselves in the stories of others.

If AI is going to take part in humanity's future, we hope it will do more than make the world faster.

We hope it will bring people a little closer—and let every language have its own starlight.
