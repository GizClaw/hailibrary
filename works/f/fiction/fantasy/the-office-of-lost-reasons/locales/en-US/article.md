# The Office of Lost Reasons

Noa hurries through the damp market. A heavy flour bag pulls at the satchel.

Noa looks for a shortcut. Under an arch, a tiny door appears.

Inside, glass bottles hold storms, clocks, and knotted ropes.

The Clerk waits beside a blank ledger. “What made you late?”

“Heavy rain,” Noa says, choosing a storm bottle.

The Clerk touches Noa's dry sleeve. “The rain missed your coat.”

Noa reaches for a clock bottle. “Then that crooked clock fooled me.”

The Clerk holds up a working watch. Noa puts the bottle back.

A third bottle shows a crowded lane. Noa remembers stopping at the pastry window.

Noa lowers the satchel. “The flour was heavy. I kept stopping because I did not want to carry it.”

The three excuse bottles burst into curls of colored smoke.

A plain bottle with a clear stone remains. The Clerk points toward the door. “There is a cart outside.”

Noa sets the heavy satchel on the handcart and grips its handles.

The cart rolls quickly to Auntie's bakery.

Noa faces Auntie. “The bag was too heavy. I should have asked for a cart.”

Auntie takes one end of the flour bag. “Next time, borrow a cart before your arms hurt.” Together, they unload it.
