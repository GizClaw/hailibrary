# The Drum on the Dragon Boat

Festival morning brought Mei to the river. She put on her safety vest and got into the boat. Today, she would drum for the crew for the first time.

Some families carried rice dumplings wrapped in leaves. Some children wore small pouches. Festival customs differ by place.

One legend says people raced boats to save Qu Yuan. He was lost in the river. They beat drums as they paddled. Other places tell other stories.

Coach Fang checked the boat and each vest. Paddlers sat in pairs, facing forward. Tao's first row set the pace. Mei drummed at the bow, while a steerer guided from the rear.

Practice began. Tao and his partner set the pace. Mei struck fast, then waited too long. The back paddles fell out of time, and the boat jerked slightly.

“I can't hear the beat!” a back-row paddler called. Mei stopped, and Tao said, “Watch my paddle.” Coach Fang said, “Match his paddle so the back rows hear the pace.”

Mei matched her drum to Tao's paddle. Her drum helped every row stay in time with his steady rhythm. The paddles entered together, and the boat went straight.

The race began. Drums sounded across the river. Mei matched Tao's rhythm, and their crew pulled together.

A wave rocked the boat, and the back paddles fell behind. Mei stayed with Tao's paddle and heard two late splashes.

Tao signaled a slower pace. Mei chose to match his paddle. Tao called to the crew: “Steady!” The late splashes moved closer to the beat.

Soon all paddles entered together. Tao quickened the pace, and Mei matched him. The boat crossed the finish line with paddles moving as one.

On shore, Tao asked, “Why did you wait before speeding up?” Mei said, “The back rows were late, so I matched your slower pace.” A distant drum marked the next race.
