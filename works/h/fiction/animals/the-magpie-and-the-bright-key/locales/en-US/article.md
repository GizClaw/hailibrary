# The Magpie and the Bright Key

At the community garden, Rowan set a bright brass key on the bench. It rested on her folded yellow gloves. Mina and Leo carried empty pots nearby.

A black-and-white magpie watched from the pear tree. The key shone below. The bird stayed high on the branch.

Rowan gathered the gloves with one hand. The key slipped into one glove, but no one saw it. She dropped the gloves into her green basket.

Later, Rowan needed the key to open the side gate. It was gone, and the magpie sat on the wall. Rowan glanced at the bird and wondered if it took the key.

Leo pointed and cried, “That magpie took the bright key!” Mina frowned. “Don’t blame the bird yet; let’s check first.”

The magpie stayed on the wall, far from the empty bench. Mina whispered, “It isn’t coming near.”

The magpie flew back to the pear tree. Mina recalled one study of wild magpies. The birds usually avoided new objects, shiny or dull.

“What did you carry after you set down the key?” Mina asked. Rowan studied her empty hands and looked toward the shed. “The gloves and my basket,” she said.

They hurried to the shed. Rowan pulled out a cloth and small tools. The folded yellow gloves lay at the bottom.

Mina lifted the gloves. The bright key slid from one glove. It landed inside the basket with a tiny clink.

Leo looked down. “I blamed the magpie because the key was bright,” he said. Rowan nodded. “So did I.”

Rowan unlocked and opened the side gate with the key. Above them, the magpie waited in the pear tree.
