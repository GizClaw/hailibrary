# The Seeds That Would Not Queue

Twelve jars of mixed seeds fill the table. Kavi will sort them all.

He shows June a wooden wheel. Three hole sizes open above three trays.

Kavi pours in round seeds. Then he turns the handle.

Small, medium, and large seeds tumble into separate trays. “It works!” Kavi cheers.

Then June brings long seeds with feathery tails.

The tails lie across the holes. The wheel jerks once and stops.

June holds up two seeds. “They are narrow, but have long tails.” Kavi looks again.

Kavi sketches a wheel with holes for every shape.

Soon the drawing looks like a maze. June taps the old wheel. “Keep its job simple.”

They change the plan. First, they group the seeds by shape.

Kavi's wheel sorts only round seeds by size.

June sorts other shapes by hand. Kavi turns the wheel.

“You can feel each tricky shape,” Kavi says. June nods. “The wheel is faster with round seeds.”

By sunset, all twelve jars are sorted. Wheel and trays rest side by side.
