# From Silkworm to Silk

Ms. Lin brings eggs for the class to raise silkworms. They hope to pull silk from their cocoons.

Days later, silkworms hatch. They will become silk moths.

The silkworms eat fresh mulberry leaves. Maya adds more leaves.

The silkworms grow, molt, and leave old skin. Owen saves one.

Soon they stop eating. They lift their heads and make silk.

Silk wraps each silkworm. A white cocoon hides it. Inside, it becomes a pupa.

Owen waits by a cocoon. At last, a silk moth crawls out.

Maya asks, “Can we pull long silk from that cocoon?” The teacher points to the broken silk.

She explains. “People usually heat cocoons before moths come out. Heat keeps moths from breaking silk, but it kills pupae.”

Maya watches the closed cocoons. “Then our pupae won’t become moths,” she whispers.

“We changed our plan,” says the teacher. “We will let our moths come out.” A silk worker brings other heated cocoons.

The worker boils other cocoons. A sticky coat holds strands, but hot water softens it. She pulls silk as the class's moths rest nearby.
