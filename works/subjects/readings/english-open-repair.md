A small part on Yue’s pencil case had broken. The zip still worked, but the piece she held had come off. She could open the case only by using the end of a pencil as a tool. One morning the pencil slipped and her ruler and two pencils slid out onto the floor of the bus. When she got home, she set the case on the kitchen table and asked Grandpa if they could fix it.

Grandpa brought out a box of tools, but the first thing he picked up was a piece of strong string. He made a small loop through the hole left by the piece that had broken off. Yue pulled the loop and the case opened. To try out the fix, she opened and closed it six more times. Her pencil could stay inside now.

## Questions

1. What had broken on Yue’s pencil case?
2. What did Grandpa use to fix the zip?

## Answers

① The little part she pulled. ② A loop made from a short piece of strong string.

At Grandpa’s work table, one leg of a small stool moved when Yue pressed on the seat. Grandpa showed her that one screw was loose. A wooden strip covered the screw head, so Grandpa removed the strip first. The strip had four short screws. Yue held a small bowl under his hands so the screws would not roll away. The loose screw was longer than the others, so she put it in a second bowl.

Grandpa fitted the leg back against the seat and tightened the long screw. Then he pressed on the stool to make sure the leg stayed still. Before Grandpa put the strip back, they counted the four short screws in the first bowl. None was missing. Yue drew the two bowls on a card and wrote FOUR SHORT, ONE LONG. They could use that trick the next time they fixed something.

## Questions

1. Why did Yue hold a bowl under Grandpa's hands?
2. Which screw stopped the leg from moving?

## Answers

① To keep the screws from rolling away. ② The long screw.

The small board where Grandpa rested his tools needed a new cover. Yue drew around the board on paper to make a pattern. She put the paper on a piece of blue cloth and cut along the line. The first cover looked right on top of the board. But when she tried to fold an edge under it, there was no cloth left to hold.

Yue made a second pattern. She drew another line a little outside each edge of the first drawing. Before cutting any more blue cloth, she used a scrap to test the new shape. It could fold under the board on all four sides. They cut the blue cloth from the larger pattern. Two wide bands held the folds under the board. To wash the cover, Yue only had to slip the bands off.

## Questions

1. Why couldn't Yue fasten the first cover?
2. What did Yue change on the paper pattern?

## Answers

① There wasn't enough cloth left to fold under the edges. ② She drew a second line outside the first one.

There was a long scratch on the top of the stool. Yue asked her aunt whether paint could hide it. Her aunt cleaned the surface with a cloth and took two small pots from a high shelf. The colors looked almost the same. Her aunt made two little marks on the back of the stool and let them dry. Only one color was a match for the old paint. “That one will match the top too,” said Yue.

Yue thought she would cover the whole scratch. Instead, her aunt smoothed the rough wood before painting it. The rough patch was gone, but she left a short pale line at the edge. She said Yue had made that scratch the first time she used the stool to reach the window. Yue ran her finger near the smooth line. “I was so little then,” she said. She could reach the window now with both feet on the floor.

## Questions

1. How did Aunt compare the two paint colors?
2. Why did she keep part of the scratch visible?

## Answers

① She made two small marks on the back and let them dry. ② It reminded her of the day Yue first used the stool.

A neighbor brought Yue a tray with a broken handle. He asked what it would cost to fix and whether he could take it home that evening. Yue found two short pieces of wood. Neither would cost anything. Grandpa asked her to think about time as well. It took a few minutes to fit a handle, but if they used glue they would have to wait for it to dry.

Yue wrote down the total time for the first plan. “An hour?” she said, looking at the total. Grandpa handed her the glue pot. The label said to leave the joint overnight before using it. An hour was far too short to promise. “The wood won't cost anything,” she told the neighbor. “I can promise to bring it back tomorrow evening.” He took an old tray home for that night and left his with Yue.

## Questions

1. What changed Yue's estimate of the time?
2. What did she promise her neighbor?

## Answers

① The glue label said to leave the joint overnight before using it. ② To bring the tray back the next evening.

Grandpa helped Yue attach a handle to the tray with screws first. The empty tray was light, but two cups would make it heavy. It felt fine when she tried to lift the empty tray. Then she asked Grandpa to set two cups on it. He suggested that they use bags of rice as weights for their first test. If the handle came off, they could pick the bags up without breaking anything. Yue put the bags in the middle and tried to lift the tray just above the work table.

The handle moved. Yue set the tray down and asked Grandpa to help her attach a wider piece of wood under the handle. They left the joint overnight, as the glue label said. The next afternoon the tray stayed level under the weight. Yue moved one bag to each side of the tray and tried once more. After that result, Yue felt ready to carry the neighbor's tray back to him with Grandpa.

## Questions

1. How did Yue test the handle without using cups?
2. What did Yue and Grandpa do when the handle moved?

## Answers

① She used bags of rice as weights. ② They attached a wider support and left the joint overnight before testing again.

Three people brought things to the work table on Saturday. Yue put parts from a small box, a tray and a toy bus into the same dish. Later she could sort the long screws from the short ones but could not tell which screws went with each object. Grandpa had taken close-up photos before the repair. They checked the screw heads in the photos. Now Yue could sort the screws by their shapes.

She wrote a label for each bag with the name of the object and its owner. Then she put a second label on the object itself. When the owner of the bus came back, his screws were in the bag with his name on it. Grandpa kept an empty bag next to the work table for each new repair. From then on, parts went straight from the object into its own bag.

## Questions

1. Why were the screws hard to match to the objects?
2. How did Yue label the bags?

## Answers

① The screws from three objects were all in one dish. ② With the name of the object and its owner.

A neighbor left a small umbrella with a cracked handle. Yue could put tape around it, but the crack would still open if someone pulled hard. Grandpa said that a new handle needed a material that would not break or hurt the person holding it. Yue did not have a suitable material, so she left the handle alone.

When the owner came back, Yue met her at the work table to explain what she had found. She also wrote a note to explain which part still needed fixing. The owner asked whether the crack meant the handle might come off. Yue pointed out how it opened when she pressed on it. She gave the note to the owner to take to a repair shop. In the record of their own repairs she drew the open crack. The drawing reminded her why she had put the tape away.

## Questions

1. Why did Yue leave the cracked umbrella handle alone?
2. Where did Yue's note go?

## Answers

① She did not have a suitable material for a new handle. ② The owner took it away to show a repair shop what needed fixing.
