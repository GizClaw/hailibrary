After the garden project, Mei’s group wanted to set up a book table for the whole school. Their first plan was to carry the books outside at lunch. But there was a problem. If it rained, there might not be enough time to put the books away.

Bo had a second plan. He thought the books would be safe by the school door. Mei walked over to see if there was enough room to pass. Two classes came through at once. That was one reason to try another spot. Bo saw a second reason when a swinging schoolbag nearly swept his book off the table. They decided to keep the books under the roof by the garden gate. The books would be safe from light rain there, and the door could stay clear. “One problem left,” said Bo, picking up his book. “Who gets to read this first?”

## Questions

1. Why was the spot by the school door a poor choice?
2. Where did the group decide to put the books on rainy days?

## Answers

① The children needed room to pass, and someone’s bag could knock the books down. ② Under the roof by the garden gate.

Tao wheeled up to the new place beside Bo and his book cart. When he tried to turn his wheelchair next to the table, he noticed that there was not enough space on one side. In Bo’s plan, the table stood in the middle so people could see the books.

Mei asked how much space they needed for a safe turn. “Let me show you,” Tao said. He made a turn in the open space and helped Mei measure it with string. They moved the table to the wall. “Choose a book,” said Tao, turning to the cart. Bo noticed that even the book cart could turn beside Tao. They agreed to keep the middle open. “Next time we have to choose a place, let's bring the cart too,” said Tao. Bo agreed. The cart was a lot easier to push than the table.

## Questions

1. What did Tao notice when he tried to turn?
2. What change did everyone agree on?

## Answers

① The space was too tight on one side of the table. ② They agreed to move the table to the wall and keep the middle open.

The group made a map to help children find the book table. Mei put a red square on the map, but a younger child could not tell what it meant. He turned the paper round. In all that white space, he had one question. “Which way do I go from here?” The map showed the place for the table, yet it did not answer the question in a way he could use.

Mei asked the child to point to the school door. He noticed that it was the same shape as every other door on the map. To explain the route, Mei drew a dotted line from the school door to the table. “That’s one example,” she said. Bo added a second example from the garden gate. They asked another reader to choose a starting place and explain her answer. She ran her finger all the way to the red square.

## Questions

1. What question did the younger child ask?
2. What example did Mei draw to explain the map?

## Answers

① Which way to the book table? ② A route from the school door to the book table.

After a night of rain, Bo tried to follow the map and stopped at a large pool of water. The old arrow pointed in the wrong direction, straight across the wet part of the path. Lin went to check the other side. At one corner the ground was dry. From that direction, children could reach the books without getting their shoes wet.

The group drew a new arrow to show the right direction. They put it at the dry corner, where children could see it from the door. To check the change, Lin asked a younger child to follow the map from the school door. He could walk across the path without stepping into the water. “Any questions about that corner?” Lin asked. His answer was a grin and two dry feet. They drew his route as an example of a safe way after rain.

## Questions

1. Why did the old arrow lead children the wrong way after rain?
2. Where did the group put the new arrow?

## Answers

① It pointed across a place with a large pool of water. ② At the dry corner, where children could see it from the door.

Once children could follow the new path, Lin set a box by the table to collect ideas. A child wanted more books about animals. Another asked a question on her slip. “When exactly are you open?” Bo added both ideas to a list on the back of a sign. Soon there were six ideas, and the group could not do them all that week.

Mei asked everyone to choose a useful order for the list. “Think of the girl who came when we were shut,” said Bo. The group agreed on an order. First they would add the opening hours to the sign, in big letters. Then they could collect a few more animal books and add them to the table. Lin moved the idea box to a corner where more people would see it. She left a blank line at the end of the list. There was room for one more idea tomorrow.

## Questions

1. What did the first two slips ask for?
2. Why did the group put the time sign first?

## Answers

① Animal books and information about opening hours. ② The sign would tell every reader when to come, including the girl who had come too late.

On Friday a child asked if she could borrow a book and take it home. The group wanted to say yes, but they did not want to lose any books. Bo suggested a simple rule. A child could take a book home and return it on the next school day, even if that meant keeping it over the weekend.

Mei made a short sign to make the rule clear. Children who wanted to borrow a book could add its title and their first name to a list. When they returned a book, they could put a small mark beside that title. Each day, Bo would collect the books from the table. The first child saw the list but missed the sign behind it. Mei moved the sign to the front. “Monday, then!” the girl said. The rule was clear. If a book seemed lost, the group could check the list and ask the reader.

## Questions

1. Why did Mei move the sign to the front?
2. What did a child need to do when returning a book?

## Answers

① She wanted the return rule to be easy to see and understand. ② Put a mark beside its title on the list.

Some children passed the table after it shut. To improve the opening hours, the group ran a small test. On Monday they opened for fifteen minutes before lunch. On Tuesday they opened for fifteen minutes after lunch instead. Bo kept a record of how many children came to the table each day.

The first result looked good for Tuesday. Four children had come before lunch on Monday, and nine after lunch on Tuesday. Lin wrote a short report. More children came to borrow or return books at the later time. But Lin's report included one more detail. It had rained on Monday, and Tuesday was dry. They wanted to see if they could improve the test. The next week they would test the times again and compare the results on days with similar weather. They kept the old opening hours instead of making a change after just two days.

## Questions

1. How did Bo compare the two opening times?
2. Why did Lin want to try the times again?

## Answers

① He counted visitors during two fifteen-minute openings, one before lunch and one after. ② More children came on Tuesday, but the weather was different. She wanted to compare the times in similar weather.

Mei’s group wanted to invite another class to run the table. The younger children knew where it was, but they did not know how to keep it going. Mei offered to explain the map and show them the results of the time test. They would need more than one class to support future readers.

The new class liked the plan. “We can take Thursdays,” one girl offered. With that support, the table could continue through the spring. They also invited older readers to bring in books that they no longer needed. Mei put the report and the last week’s record in the red box, along with a blank page. She wrote a note at the top about where to return the box. The rest of the page was for future records and ideas. “Can we continue this list?” the girl asked. Mei passed her the pencil. Under Thursday, the girl wrote her name.

## Questions

1. How did the new group offer to support the book table?
2. What was the rest of the page for?

## Answers

① They offered to run it on Thursdays. ② The new group’s records and ideas.
