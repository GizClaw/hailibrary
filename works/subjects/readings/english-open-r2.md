Lin and Bo were carrying books from the class shop. On their way back they saw a little green plant by the school garden. It was growing in the ground next to a busy path. Bo went back to bring a box of small stones. To help others see the plant, they put the stones on the ground around it. At lunch they went to watch from the garden gate. A boy with a football stopped at the circle and walked around it. “Let's watch the next one,” said Bo.

## Questions

1. What did the children bring to mark the small plant?
2. Why did they put stones around the plant?

## Answers

① A box of small stones. ② So others would see it and walk around it.

Bo put two cups of dry garden soil on the step. He put a lid on one and left the other open. When the rain came, he watched from the door. The soil under the lid stayed dry. The soil in the open cup got wet. Bo found a third cup and caught some rain in it. “Free water for our plant!” he called. He felt the soil in the plant pot. It wasn't wet yet, so he used the fresh water there.

## Questions

1. Why was the soil inside one cup dry?
2. Which cup held the water Bo gave the plant?

## Answers

① Its lid kept the rain out. ② The third cup, which he put out empty.

A dark shadow covered the plant by the garden wall when Lin came to school. Lin could see the sun above the school, but the wall kept its light away from that part of the garden. She went back more than once because she wanted to watch the light change. After lunch the sun lit up the wall. A patch of light reached the little plant too. By home time a dark shadow lay across its leaves again. Lin drew the wall three times and used yellow to show each change.

## Questions

1. When did light reach the plant by the wall?
2. Why did Lin go back to look more than once?

## Answers

① After lunch. ② She wanted to watch how the light changed.

Lin wanted to measure the little plant without pulling on it. She held a strip of paper straight up beside the stem. The bottom of the paper touched the ground. She made a line to mark the top of the plant. She took the paper inside to keep it dry. A week later, she brought the same paper back to the garden. Before lunch, she put the bottom of the paper in the same place and made a new mark. The new line was above the old one. She held the paper up for Bo. “Can you measure the space between my two lines?”

## Questions

1. Where did Lin put the bottom of the paper each time?
2. How did the two lines show that the plant grew?

## Answers

① On the ground beside the plant. ② The new line was above the old one.

Bo wanted to see which way the wind was blowing. He took a short stick and tied a piece of string to the top. Lin held the stick up beside the garden. When the wind was strong, the string went out to one side. Bo made a mark for the stick on their map and drew a line from it towards the gate. The wind died down and the string fell. Then the wind grew strong again. This time the string turned towards the wall. “Hold it there,” Bo said. Starting at the same mark, he drew another line towards the wall. You could see the turn in the wind on the page.

## Questions

1. Where did the string point the second time?
2. How did Bo show both directions on the map?

## Answers

① Towards the wall. ② He drew two lines from the mark for the stick, one towards the gate and one towards the wall.

In a light rain, Lin stood under the roof near the garden wall. Bo stood far from the wall with an umbrella. Lin asked Bo to look down at the same time she did. They wanted to compare the ground in two different places during the same rain. Beyond his umbrella, Bo saw wet spots join up. Lin saw dry ground near the wall, where the roof was over it. When they came inside, each drew a picture of what they saw. “How far from the wall were you?” Lin asked. They wrote their places on the pictures. The ground looked so different that Mei put them side by side to compare them.

## Questions

1. Which place stayed dry in the light rain?
2. Why did the children look in both places at the same time?

## Answers

① The ground near the wall under the roof. ② So they could compare the places during the same rain.

Lin wanted to record what happened to the shadow of the garden wall in one hour. At the start of the hour, she marked the end of the shadow in chalk and copied the line onto paper. At the end of the hour, she drew a second line. Both lines were black. Later, it was hard to tell which mark came first because the lines looked the same. The next day, Lin went out at the same time and started a new drawing. This time she wrote the time by each mark as she made it. Her record now showed which way the shadow moved in the light of the sun.

## Questions

1. What did Lin record at the start and end of the hour?
2. Why did Lin put the times on her new drawing?

## Answers

① Where the wall's shadow ended. ② Without the times, she could not tell which line came first.

Lin brought their garden records to the class shop. Her group had too many pages for the small table, so they had to decide what to show first. “Can we keep my picture of the wind?” Bo asked. Mei wanted to show how light could change in an hour. The group put the two lines from the shadow drawing on the first page. They kept the times by the lines. The next group could draw the shadow twice, an hour apart, and compare their drawing with Lin's. They decided to keep all the other pages in the red box.

## Questions

1. What did the group decide to put on the first page?
2. How could the next group use Lin's page?

## Answers

① The two lines from the shadow drawing and their times. ② They could draw the shadow twice, an hour apart, and compare their drawing with hers.
