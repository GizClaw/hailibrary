On Saturday, Tavi sat outside Aunt Nessa’s shop to tie his shoes. The morning sun was low. The handle on the blue door made a little black shape, and the flowerpot had a round shadow. A little tin foot brushed his shoe. By the time he stood up, the step was empty. So was the sunny patch where his shadow should have been.

He took two steps into the street. Still nothing. Then he saw a small fox coming round the corner. It was made of tin, a kind of metal, and one ear was bent. Behind it was the shadow of a boy with one shoe untied. Tavi looked down at his own shoe.

“Wait!” he called. The fox stopped. The shadow stopped too, although Tavi was still walking. It wanted to follow the fox!

Aunt Nessa came to the door with her cup of tea. “I thought I heard little feet last night,” she said. Tavi pointed to the black shape. The fox put a paw on the black shape. Tavi could see a little knot in one shadow shoe. He still had to tie the other one.

## Questions

1. What was wrong when Tavi stood in the sun?
2. Finish the sentence with up to three words. The shadow was following a ___ .

## Answers

1. He could see the shadows of other things, but his own shadow was gone.
2. little tin fox

Aunt Nessa listened while Tavi told her about the fox. Then she set her cup down by the blue door. “Toys left by this door at night sometimes wake up,” she said. “If a fox touches your shoe on the sunny step, your shadow can follow it. I saw it happen once before.”

“Can I get it back now?” asked Tavi.

“Come back together before the sun leaves this step. Stand side by side on the step. With the door shut, knock three times. Then your shadow will go with you again.”

The fox looked at the blue door. “My sister needs me,” it said in a voice like a spoon tapping a cup. “She's up high. I thought your shadow could reach her.”

Tavi looked at his shadow. Outside the shop it was taller than Aunt Nessa. “I’ll come too,” he said. He tied his shoe at last and ran to catch up with the fox.

## Questions

1. What must Tavi and the fox do before the sun leaves the step?
2. Why doesn’t Tavi try to take the shadow back right away?

## Answers

1. They must stand together on the step and knock three times with the door shut.
2. The fox says it has a job to do and needs the shadow’s help. Tavi agrees to go with it.

The fox took Tavi to a yellow house. “My sister is in that window,” it said. On the window shelf sat a toy train. A second tin fox with a blue tail sat in the driver's seat. A little boy was putting his toys in a big box by the window.

The fox called and jumped as high as it could. Up in the house, the boy went on putting things in the box. The window was shut, and from there he couldn’t see the little fox down in the street.

“Try over here,” said Tavi. He walked to the other side of the narrow street. The fox stood opposite the window with the sun behind it. Now the boy-shaped shadow could reach the white blind. The fox waved a paw. From inside the room the boy saw a big hand wave on the blind. He looked out. The fox pointed to the train. The boy took her into his arms and ran downstairs.

## Questions

1. Why can’t the fox ask the little boy for help by itself?
2. How does the fox use the shadow to get the boy’s attention?

## Answers

1. The window is shut, and the boy inside cannot hear the tiny fox or see it down below.
2. With the sun behind it, the fox waves a paw. The shadow waves a big hand on the white blind, where the boy can see it.

A door opened, and the little boy came out carrying the fox with the blue tail. “Those two foxes belong to me!” he said. “Aunt Nessa painted their stars last week. They both spent a night by her blue door. I brought the little one back yesterday about its bent ear.”

Tavi had seen Aunt Nessa fix toys before. She sometimes left them by the door for their owners to collect. “So that's why I found you on her step,” he said to the little fox.

“My cousin likes trains,” the boy told him. “I’m giving him my old train and some other toys. I put the blue fox in the train this morning. I didn’t know one fox could miss the other.” The little fox stood very still. The boy looked at the two tin faces. They were almost the same, and each one had a small white star on its nose.

“Oh,” he said. “You two belong together.” “The train can go to my cousin. You two can live on my shelf.” He brought the train down and set both foxes in it. “You can sit here while I pack.”

## Questions

1. Who was going to get the box of toys?
2. Why does the boy keep both foxes out of the box?

## Answers

1. The little boy’s cousin, who liked trains.
2. He realises the foxes belong together and wants to keep them both on his shelf.

While the boy packed his cousin's toys, Tavi watched the shadow of a roof move across the street. From the corner he could just see the shop. The patch of sun on Aunt Nessa's step was getting smaller.

“We need to hurry,” he told the fox with the bent ear. The fox climbed down from the train. The shadow slid down the side of the toy engine with it.

“Can your sister come with us?” asked Tavi. The little fox looked at its sister. The boy held up a brown bag. “My cousin’s toys are ready. I can come now and carry her.”

Tavi turned towards the corner, but the boy opened a side gate. “This lane goes right behind the shop,” he said. “It’s shorter.” They hurried down the lane, past the place where the boy kept his bike. At the far end Tavi could already see Aunt Nessa carrying a chair in through the back door.

## Questions

1. What makes Tavi want to hurry?
2. Which way does the boy show them, and why do they take it?

## Answers

1. He sees the patch of sun getting smaller on the shop step. They need to get back while part of the step is still sunny.
2. He opens the side gate to a lane. It is a shorter way to the back of Aunt Nessa’s shop.

Aunt Nessa saw Tavi in the lane and put down her chair. “Round to the front!” she called. “There’s still a little sun on the step.”

They ran round the shop. The sunlight was right at one end of the step now. It was only as wide as Tavi’s hand. The fox with the bent ear had its paws in the sun. Tavi stood beside it on the step. The boy with the brown bag shut the blue door for them.

Tavi knocked once. The boy-shaped shadow still stretched out from the fox's feet. He knocked again, and the shadow shook a little. On the third knock the shadow slipped across the step like water. Tavi lifted his left hand. The shadow lifted its hand too.

The fox tried to wave at its sister. A tiny fox shadow waved too. The big hand was gone. The fox looked down for a long time. Then it pointed to the white star on its sister’s nose. She touched the star on its nose with her paw.

## Questions

1. Where is the last bit of sunlight when they reach the front of the shop?
2. What shows that the trick has worked?

## Answers

1. At one end of the front step.
2. The boy-shaped shadow moves with Tavi again. It lifts its hand when he lifts his.

The boy put down the brown bag. On one side he had drawn a train with four round wheels. “That’s for my cousin,” he said. “I can draw a little fox too.”

“Could you help me make a big one?” asked Tavi. Aunt Nessa gave them some dark paper and turned on the lamp by her worktable. The boy drew a fox with a bent ear and cut it out. Tavi held it near the lamp. A much bigger fox appeared on the wall.

They put a big white sheet of paper on the wall, held the small fox close to the lamp and drew round its shadow. The line went up to the high ears and down around the long tail. The tin fox watched from the edge of the table. When Tavi switched off the lamp, the big shadow disappeared. The drawn fox stayed. It had a bent ear exactly like the little fox’s.

## Questions

1. Where does Tavi get the idea for making a fox shape?
2. How do Tavi and the boy make the outline bigger?

## Answers

1. He sees the boy’s drawing on the brown bag and asks him to help make a bigger picture.
2. They put a lamp near the paper fox so that its shadow on the wall looks big, then draw round it.

When Tavi came to the yellow house the next morning, the boy was waiting by the door. “Come and see where they sleep,” he said. A little train stood on the shelf by his bed. It had two seats. The boys had made it out of paper the night before, after the toy train went home with the cousin.

The fox with the bent ear sat in front, its sister just behind it. On the wall behind them was the picture of the fox with the bent ear. Next to it the boy had drawn another one with a blue tail. “These two get a night train too,” he said. He picked up a pencil and drew two more wheels under the paper foxes. The boy moved the picture a little so it hung straight.

On his way home, Tavi saw his own shadow on the yellow wall. He waved, and the shadow waved too. Just for a moment he thought he saw two little faces in the high window, with two white stars very close together.

## Questions

1. What did the boys make for the two foxes to sit in?
2. What detail turns the drawing into a night train for the paper foxes?

## Answers

1. A paper train with two seats.
2. The boy draws wheels under them so they can ride together too.
