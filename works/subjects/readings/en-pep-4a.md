After PE on Friday I’m tired enough to sleep on the bus. On Saturday it’s Mum’s turn to sleep late. She’s a nurse and was busy at the hospital all night. Dad, an office worker, takes his noisy phone into another room. ‘What’s my job?’ I whisper to Grandpa.

‘Find a quiet chore,’ he says. I sweep the kitchen floor. We work together. My sister and I look after the soup while Grandpa gets the table ready. We often cook lunch with him. When he was a child, he helped his father, a farmer, cook for the people who worked on the farm. My aunt, a doctor, comes for lunch. She peeks into the clean kitchen. ‘Where’s Mum?’ We put our fingers to our lips.

**Mina**　Can we go out after lunch on Sunday?

**Dad**　Yes. I’ll sweep the floor first. Can you look after your little brother?

**Mina**　Sure. Is Aunt May coming with us?

**Dad**　She’s a factory worker. Her shift starts at two.

**Mina**　Could we cook her lunch together before we go?

**Dad**　Let’s do that. You get the fruit. I’ll make the sandwiches.

I always play football with Ben on Friday. Today the field is wet, and even Ben’s hair is dripping. We try a table game instead. There’s a quiet boy at the next table. His name is Qiao. He can read the rules in Chinese and shows us where our pieces go.

Ben is strong and good at basketball, but he keeps pushing his piece right off the board. Qiao puts one finger on his own piece. ‘Like this.’ Ben tries again. This time it stops in just the right place. Later, Qiao tells us about a basketball court with a roof. Next Friday, rain or shine, he has a place on our team.

On Saturday afternoon my cousin and I wait at the bus stop near the hospital. There is a big map on the side of the shelter. ‘Our community!’ says my cousin. I find a shop, a library and a park. A man wants to know where the playground is. We can point it out on the map.

We buy some paper at the shop and walk to the park to make our own map. There’s a place for sport over by the gate, with a toilet nearby. My favourite spot is the bench where Grandpa tells me a story while we wait for the bus. I draw him on our map. My cousin takes a photo to show him.

Ms Ma gives us a blank wall and a box of pins. ‘Who helped you get here this morning?’ she asks. I draw our bus driver. Rui draws the cleaner who moved a fallen branch off the pavement. Our neighbour is a delivery worker. I draw her holding the door for me with her elbow. Her hands are full of boxes.

Now there are a lot of pictures. A firefighter and a police officer come to see them. ‘Why is there a bed on the wall?’ asks the firefighter. I tell him about my old grandpa. Every morning I help him make the bed. Today he’s drawn a picture of me. For Grandpa, my help is important too. Now everyone who stops at the wall can see his picture of me!

Our friends in Sydney call on Friday. We speak to them from our classroom. Their playground is hot and sunny. They hope to swim tomorrow. We hold our phone up to the window. The sky here is cloudy. A cold wind is pulling leaves off the trees. ‘Kite weather!’ says Dai.

We have two plans for Saturday. If it’s windy and dry, we’ll go to the park to fly a kite. If the rain comes back, we’ll stay warm at home and watch a film about a snowman. ‘A snowy film for a rainy day,’ says Dai. The park is closed today. I’ll check the gate before we set off tomorrow.

We stretch a line across our classroom. Each part of the line is for a season. We hang up pictures of clothes. I put a coat, a sweater and a pair of socks under ‘winter’. In winter, when it starts to snow here, I wear all three. Li adds a dress and a green leaf for spring.

There’s a thin shirt and a T-shirt for summer and a jacket for autumn, when the leaves turn yellow and fall. Then we get together to look at the line. ‘Whose glove is this?’ says Li. She’s found a real wool glove under the winter pictures. ‘It’s mine!’ I say. ‘It can stay on show until it’s time to go home.’

**Tao**　Whose sweater is this? Is it yours?

**Mei**　Yes, it’s mine. Which coat are you taking to the hills?

**Tao**　The warm one. It might snow up there. Shall I take a T-shirt too?

**Mei**　Yes. You can wear a summer shirt under a sweater. Pack dry socks too.

**Tao**　And my gloves? I can only find one.

**Mei**　Feel in your coat pocket. There’s the other one!
