Lu found a small radio in the back of his grandfather’s cupboard. Grandpa said that the radio had sat by their old shop door. “People knew which house they were passing by the sounds,” he said. Lu wondered which local sounds had gone quiet since the shop shut. He could interview the people who remembered them. “May I record your voice later, Grandpa? I'd like to hear one memory first.” He put the radio down and listened.

Grandpa spoke about a metal bell. The owner of the shop next door remembered a wooden cart instead. Lu wanted to know which memory was right. His cousin Xi said that the two men could be describing different hours of the day. They wrote down both sounds and began a list of questions. Next time they met a local shopkeeper, they had a way to start the interview. “Talk me through your morning. What could you hear from the door?”

## Questions

1. What did Lu want to interview local people about?
2. What two different details did he hear first?

## Answers

① Sounds their street used to have. ② A metal bell and the sound of a wooden cart.

Xi wanted to record Grandma talking about the street. Grandma saw the small recorder on the table and stopped before she spoke. She asked whether the recording would be private or whether everyone at the school could hear it. Xi had planned to make a public show. She put the recorder down and asked permission to record a practice version that only Grandma and the two children would hear.

Grandma was happy to share the sound of the old bell. She wanted the name of the friend who rang it to stay private. The children agreed. “You can record now,” said Grandma. She told the story of the bell and called the girl “my friend”. Later Xi played it to her and asked for permission again. “That bit can go in the public show,” Grandma said. Xi wrote the decision beside the name of the sound.

## Questions

1. Why did Grandma stop before speaking?
2. What did the children agree to keep private?

## Answers

① She wanted to know who could hear the recording. ② The name of her old friend.

Lu brought two photos to his group to compare. In one photo there was a square window above a shop. The other showed a window with only half its frame in view. He wanted to use the square window in their show as a mark for the bell sound. Xi asked him to check a detail first. Both photos showed the same brick with a white mark under the window.

The two pictures could be of the same shop, but Lu could date only one. The shop owner had dated the second photo on the back. He remembered taking it the summer he put up the awning. No one knew the date of the first. Lu wrote “exact date unknown” next to that picture. Lu made small copies of the photos and circled the brick on each copy. That detail gave visitors something they could check for themselves.

## Questions

1. Which detail suggested that the pictures showed the same place?
2. How did Lu label the date of the first photo?

## Answers

① Both photos showed the same brick with a white mark. ② He marked the first photo as having no exact date.

Lu asked two neighbors about the shop bell. One said it rang at six at night. The other said seven. Lu looked for another source. A photo showed the bell clearly, but there was no clock in the picture. That was evidence that the bell had been there. It gave him no evidence of what time anyone rang it.

Xi suggested putting both memories on the display. Lu wrote that the exact time was uncertain and gave the date of each interview. When Lu read the first source again, a detail caught his eye. One neighbor had heard the bell on the way home from evening classes. The other had been out after dinner. There might have been more than one bell sound a day. At the bottom Xi wrote, “Did someone ring the bell twice?” They still needed evidence before they could give an exact answer.

## Questions

1. Why did Lu say the time of the evening bell was uncertain?
2. How did they present the two sources?

## Answers

① The neighbours remembered different times, and the photo showed no clock. ② They put both accounts on the display and labeled the uncertain time.

The old shopkeeper found the bell at home. With his permission, Xi recorded him ringing it. She wanted everyone in the audience to know the sound, including people who couldn’t hear it. She tried to express what she heard in a few words on a card. Her first line was “It was a nice bell.” Lu said that did not tell him what it sounded like. Xi listened again. This time she described the sound with a line about two small rings close together, then a long quiet wait.

“It sounds like a spoon tapping a tin lid,” one visitor said. Xi borrowed his example, with his permission, to express how bright the little rings were. She asked one person in the audience to read both cards before listening. The second card helped him describe the sound in his own words. They took down the “nice bell” card. Visitors could now choose between a description of the two rings and the comparison with a tin lid.

## Questions

1. How did Xi describe the bell sound?
2. Why did another description help the audience?

## Answers

① As two small rings close together, then a long quiet wait. ② The comparison to a spoon tapping a tin lid gave visitors a familiar sound to imagine.

Lu and Xi had to arrange the sounds on three boards. They put the oldest memory in the first section. The next board showed newer, dated photos, and the last one held the latest recordings. A visitor followed the cards and asked where to stand to hear the sound from the bridge. He could see the dates, but he could not tell how to walk along the street.

Xi asked what the main theme of their show was. The children had wanted to help visitors hear the street as they walked along it. They decided to arrange the boards by place. Each section showed one part of the street and one old or new sound. An arrow led from the picture of the shop door to the section about the bridge. “I get the theme now,” the visitor said. “It's like taking a walk. Where do I go after the bridge?”

## Questions

1. What was hard to follow in the first display?
2. How did the children arrange the new display?

## Answers

① The dates were clear, but the cards didn't show the route along the street. ② They grouped the sounds by place and added simple arrows.

The group planned to print a page about the finished show. Just before they did, a visitor said there was a mistake on one card. It said a street photo was taken in the year the new shop opened. The visitor showed Xi another copy of the same photo. On the back was a note saying it was taken two years later. “I took it myself,” the visitor said. Xi asked when she had dated the print. “That very afternoon.”

Xi checked that the pictures matched before correcting the date on the card. Lu asked if the correct date was all they needed to show. Xi remembered that some people had seen the show the previous day. She put a short note beside the new date so those visitors could see how the mistake had happened. They added the same note to the page they would print. Under the picture went the correct year and the name of the photographer who had helped them find it.

## Questions

1. How did Xi check the visitor’s correction?
2. Why did she keep a note beside the new date?

## Answers

① She compared the two pictures and asked the photographer when she had written the dated note. ② Earlier visitors might remember the old date and wonder why it changed.

The show was nearly over. Xi and Lu wanted to preserve the sounds after the boards came down. For each recording in their collection they kept a description and a note of who had given permission to share it. They left one blank line on the last page for a future contribution. They did not know whether anyone would use that space.

On the last day a child came in with a recording of the public courtyard very early in the morning. “I thought it was silent,” she said. When they played it, they heard a bird, then a bicycle bell far away. The child wanted the collection to preserve the quiet seconds too. With her permission, they kept her whole contribution. Lu put on the headphones for one last listen. This time he heard the bicycle long before the bell.

## Questions

1. Why did the children leave an empty line in the archive?
2. What did a child contribute on the last day?

## Answers

① For sounds that neighbors might bring later. ② An early-morning courtyard recording with quiet gaps, a bird and a distant bicycle bell.
