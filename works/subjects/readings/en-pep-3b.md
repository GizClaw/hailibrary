Today a new student comes to our class. Her name is Tara. ‘Where are you from?’ asks Nan, my classmate. Tara is from Canada. She and Nan talk in English. Tara writes her home town on a card and pins it to the map on the wall. After class we take a closer look.

There are about ten cards now, some from China, one from the UK and one from the USA. A boy points to a card by the sea. ‘Who put that one there?’ Our teacher, Mr Chen, puts up his hand. He has a photo of his old school. A girl helps him pin it beside his card.

**Nan**　My neighbour. The man beside her is my dad.

**Tara**　Does your neighbour speak English?

**Nan**　Yes. She’s from the UK. She helps me read my English books.

**Tara**　That’s very kind. Can I show her our map today? There are about ten cards on it.

**Nan**　Come and ask her! I think she’ll spot her home town on it.

Lan and I make cards for Family Day. In her picture a dog has a long body, short legs and a thin tail. It has a round face. Beside him is a fat cat. ‘Is the tail right?’ I ask. ‘It’s so long!’ ‘Let’s make it stand up,’ I say. Lan folds a piece of paper and sticks it on the back.

Mum is coming to hear our song. Lan stands the card by her chair. We sing and dance and give out our cards. Lan’s mum puts her gift on the table. The cat and the dog look up at her. All the other cards lie flat. ‘Mine can stand!’ she says.

In class we learn about our senses. An apple sits on a plate. I can see its shape and feel the smooth skin when I touch it. I use my ruler to measure it and draw it on paper with my pencil. I keep my pen for the name under my picture. Our teacher cuts it up. We wash our hands. Now I can smell the apple with my nose and taste the juice on my tongue.

‘Listen!’ says my friend. I hear a crunch as he bites his slice. I try a bite too. Crunch! After class we find a picture of an apple tree in a book. Our teacher uses her computer to show us its white flowers.

At home my old toy boat sits on a shelf. My brother wants to play with it. I put a blue map in the bottom of a box. ‘Here’s your sea,’ I say. He puts on his cap. Now he’s the captain. I find my toy car under the bed and sit a doll in it.

I drive the doll to the box. ‘All aboard!’ calls my brother. The doll goes on the boat. The ball of yellow paper in my hand is the sun. When the game is over, we keep the boat in the box. Tomorrow our little sea will still be there.

Dad finds a piece of blue card in an old box. We draw a train on it with eleven wheels and thirteen windows. My sister puts a face in each window. She wants to add our dog, too. ‘He needs window number fourteen!’ I say. There’s just enough room at the back of our train.
