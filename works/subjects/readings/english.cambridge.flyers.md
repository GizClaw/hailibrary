My aunt was coming to stay with us for the weekend. While Dad and my aunt were carrying bags in from the car, I decided to tidy the little room by myself. I put the books on the bottom shelf, pulled a chair up to the window and looked for the soft green cushion that goes on it. The room looked tidy, but the cushion was missing.

I thought my brother was alone in the garden. When I called, he opened the back door just a little. A small dog was lying on the cushion behind him, its nose between its paws. My brother had found the dog outside our gate. He was looking after it until somebody came for it.

A few minutes later, Dad and my aunt came in. There was a surprise for all of us. The dog jumped up and ran straight to her. It was her new dog! It had slipped through the open car door while they were carrying her bags. That evening, I found a second cushion for the chair. “Sorry the room looked so untidy,” I told my aunt. She laughed. For our science project, our group had to design a bridge between two desks. A toy car had to cross it. I wanted to use a long piece of plastic, but Rosa thought we should try wood. We tried one bridge with just plastic, then added wood to see what would change. You can try it too.

First, put the plastic across the desks. Roll the car slowly across. Does the plastic bend in the middle? Mark where its ends rest on the desks. If the car is too heavy for the plastic, try adding two pieces of wood underneath. Use a small metal clip at each end to keep things in place. Put the ends back on the marks and test with the same car. Now you can compare the two designs.

At the end of the lesson, show a friend your first design and your new one. Explain what each material does and how it helped you improve the bridge. If you need to repair the bridge after every test, try moving the desks closer together.

We waited on a seat near the path. While we were waiting, I noticed a little insect on my coat. It had two long wings. I put it down on a leaf, and it flew away. Then the fog began to get thinner. Our view of the pond changed. First we saw the water, and then two white heads appeared near the far side. The swans moved slowly past us.

Our club leader asked us why we had stayed on the path. “Because this is their home,” I said. On my way back, I wrote that down. We had stayed close enough to watch two wild swans and far enough away to leave the grass by their pond alone. I drew that part of their environment in my notebook.

Our school held a music competition in the little theatre near the park. My friend Bea and I wanted to enter it. I played a tune on my violin, and she played a drum. We made our own costumes and practised every day. But when we got to the theatre, Bea looked in her bag. Her little hand drum was missing.

We had only a minute before we had to go on stage. “Play this,” I whispered. Our costumes had come in a stiff cardboard box. I turned it upside down and handed it to her. She hit it with her hands. It sounded like a very quiet drum. When I began the tune, she listened and played along. The whole theatre went quiet, and everybody could hear us.

We didn’t win the first prize. The winner played two instruments in the same song. But after the competition, a young boy asked Bea how to play a box. We showed him and he tried it. Later we heard him teach his sister the same thing at the bus stop.
