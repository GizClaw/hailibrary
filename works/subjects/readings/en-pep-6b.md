My younger brother and I make model dinosaurs for a show in our school hall. His dinosaur is taller and heavier than mine. Mine is thinner and has a longer tail, but his dinosaur has stronger legs. We build a model museum out of a box one metre wide. The first door I cut is smaller than we need. My brother’s dinosaur is too tall to go through.

‘A bigger door?’ I ask. He has a smarter idea. He gives his dinosaur a shorter stand made of thinner wood. The model sits lower now. It fits! Now both models can go through. We shine a torch at them and a huge dinosaur shadow appears on the back wall.

**Bei:** How tall is the dinosaur your older sister made?

**Sam:** It's almost one metre tall. The new one is shorter.

**Bei:** How heavy is the new one?

**Sam:** About one kilogram. It's lighter, so we can take it to the village show.

**Bei:** The show in the countryside? Is there room for both models in the car?

**Sam:** I think so. The taller one can lie on its side.

**Bei:** Good idea. Let’s wrap a blanket round the tail so it won’t bump against the other model.

**Sam:** I’ll bring one. A box and a blanket can become a cosy dinosaur bed!

Last Saturday we stayed at Grandma's house. She had a cold and slept late. Dad washed the cups, and I cleaned the table. Before lunch I read a magazine. It showed how to make a game from a box. I fixed a broken box with tape and put three upside-down cups inside it. Grandma watched me hide a red button under one cup.

I slid the cups around. ‘That one!’ Grandma lifted the middle cup. There was the button. Now it was my turn to guess. Yesterday, she felt much better. We drank tea and played again. She slid the cups so fast that I lost track of the one with the button.

**Ivy:** What did you do last weekend?

**Jin:** We watched a show in the town hall. Dad saw a sign for it at our hotel.

**Ivy:** How long did you stay? Did you enjoy the show?

**Jin:** Yes. A woman made rain sounds by shaking a box of beans. She shook it faster when the rain got heavy.

**Ivy:** What happened next?

**Jin:** Another person used a drum. One loud roll and it sounded just like thunder. Then they turned off the desk lamp on the stage, and a paper moon came out.

**Ivy:** Can we make those sounds for our class story?

**Jin:** Yes! I can bring a box of beans. Will you stay after class and be in charge of the thunder?

**Mia:** Did you have to stay there till the others came back?

**Lei:** No. Back at the farm, Dad made up a play. He gave me the part of the farmer and tried to sound like a mule. The real mule had its nose in a basket of carrots!

**Mia:** Did the mule get a carrot?

**Lei:** Yes! I caught it on camera just as it licked its lips. We were home before dark, so I showed Mum too. Want to see?

Last Friday our class opened an old box from a shelf at school. The label said ‘Gate’. Inside we found a map and a long piece of blue string. The map showed our school twenty years ago. There was no gym then, and the playground was smaller. On Saturday, we went back to see if the old gate was still there. It was behind a tree.

We held one end of the string against the left post and tied a knot at the right post. We lined up the same end at the new gate. The knot went past the right post. The old gate was wider. We took pictures of both and marked the two gates on our map. On Monday, we put our pictures in the box and wrote a note for the next class.

**Jia:** What did you put in the box yesterday?

**Ivy:** A new map and the photos we took. I wrote our names and the dates on the back of each photo.

**Jia:** Is the new map bigger than the old one?

**Ivy:** It's smaller, but it shows more of the school. There wasn’t a garden on the map from twenty years ago.

**Jia:** Can we write about what we did in the garden last week?

**Ivy:** Yes. We planted beans and gave them water. One day there may be a bigger garden.

**Jia:** Where shall we leave the box?

**Ivy:** In the school office by the gate. Write the year on the lid so the next class can find our map.
