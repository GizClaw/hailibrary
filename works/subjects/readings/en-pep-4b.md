On Monday our class gets a new rule. The last person out of the classroom must turn off the light and the fan. We write the rule on the blackboard. When we go across the yard for music, I look back through the window. Our empty room is still bright. Now I understand why we need a sign.

I fold a piece of newspaper, stick white paper on it and draw a light and a fan. Niu helps me put it on the wall by the door. At the end of the day our desks are tidy and our chairs are in place. Niu is ready to leave. He spots the sign. Click, click. This time the room is quiet and dark.

Friday afternoon is loud at our house. My sister and I play in the living room. My brother likes to do homework in the study. Mum comes home from the hospital. She has to work hard on the night shift. All she wants to do is sleep.

In the kitchen, my sister and I think of a plan. First we turn down the TV. Then we take our game into my bedroom. My brother finishes his work in the quiet and comes to join us. Before our snack we wash our hands in the bathroom. Mum wakes up to find three children buttering toast. ‘That was a helpful idea,’ she says. ‘I feel much better.’ We save her the first slice.

Our class is going on a trip. I make a list of clothes to take. My shorts will be fine in the sun. For rain I’ll need a jacket. Mum adds a note at the bottom. ‘Dear Ming, try on the jacket over your sweater.’ She knows I hate tight sleeves.

I wear my thick trousers and my old walking shoes to the shop. My sister tries a beautiful skirt and a pair of shorts. It’s too expensive, so she puts it back. I find a large jacket, but the sleeves cover my hands. The next size down is just right. There’s room for my sweater. ‘I’ll take it,’ I tell the woman at the till. She gives me a free paper bag. Outside, the sun comes out. My sister takes her sunglasses out of her pocket and grins. I can keep my jacket in the bag for now.

We follow our guide through a small gate on the farm. A cow and a horse are drinking from a long trough. Beyond it, a sheep stands in the shade. I hear a pig in the next pen. A chicken runs across the path just ahead of us.

The guide stops at a second gate. There are tomato plants and rows of carrots and potatoes on the other side. A mouse slips behind an empty can by the wall. ‘Did you see that?’ I whisper. My partner is watching a bee on a flower and misses the mouse. We swap places. She can just see its tail. Before we leave, the guide gives us a box of green beans to take back to school.

Every week our group helps in the school garden. Today we pick beans and tomatoes. The cook makes soup with the beans and a salad with the tomatoes. I use a spoon to taste the soup. Delicious! There are thirty bowls on the shelf. I take twenty and help set the table. We need forks for the salad, chopsticks for the noodles and a knife for the bread.

My card says ‘Lunch table fourteen’. The tables have numbers on them, but I’m too short to see the signs from the door. An older girl walks down the row with me. ‘One, two, three, four, five,’ we count. Six, seven, eight, nine, ten. We turn the corner. Eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen. Here’s my seat! She walks on past fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen and nineteen. Her table is number twenty.

After lunch the two of us help count clean cups. Each tray holds ten. We count together as the trays go on the shelf. ‘Ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty.’ Halfway there! ‘Sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety, one hundred.’ The last tray fits. I can’t see the top of the shelf, but I know just how many cups are up there.
