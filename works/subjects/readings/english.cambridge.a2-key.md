On Monday, Mia saw a notice next to the school library door. The book club met there every Thursday. New students could join, and they did not need to bring a book. Mia wanted to go, but she was slow at reading in English. She did not know any members of the club either.

At lunch, she asked the teacher at the library desk about it. He gave her a form and showed her the shelf where she could borrow a book. “Try it for a week,” he said. Mia filled in the form and chose a short story about a dog. “You can return it after Thursday's meeting,” he told her. That gave Mia three evenings to practise the part she liked best.

When Mia arrived on Thursday, the only spare chair had a schoolbag on it. A boy called Ben lifted the bag onto his knees. “Here you go.” The students did not read to the whole room. They worked in pairs, so Mia and Ben could take turns reading. Mia read one line and passed the book to Ben. When her turn came again, she kept it for three lines. On her way out, Mia saw a student looking at the notice. This time, she was the one who said, “Come and sit next to me next week.”

**Noah.** Ella, are you a member of the book club? I saw a notice on the library door, but I didn’t have time to read it all.

**Ella.** Yes. Our next meeting is on Thursday at four. Do you want to join?

**Noah.** I think so. I’d like to practise reading aloud, but I don’t want to read in front of everyone.

**Ella.** You can sit with me. We take turns reading, just the two of us.

**Noah.** That sounds good. Do I need my own book?

**Ella.** No, you can borrow one here today and return it after Thursday's meeting.

**Noah.** And how do I put my name down?

**Ella.** Fill in this form and leave it at the desk. Then choose your book. The one with the yellow cover is funny.

**Noah.** Thanks. I can try it for a week and see if I like it.

On Saturday morning, Imani and Leo put twenty old books on a table at the school fair. They wanted to sell the books and use the money for new plants in the school garden. Leo made a sign. It said each book would cost one pound.

Their first customer was a girl with a green bag. She wanted to buy two books, one for her brother and one for herself. She only had one pound. She put one of the books back and stood counting the coins in her hand. Imani looked at the table. There were still a lot of books, and the fair was only open for two hours.

“Why don’t we change the price?” Imani asked. “We can sell one book for fifty pence, or two for a pound.” Leo wrote a new sign. Now the books were cheap enough for the girl to take both. She picked up the second book again and gave Imani her pound. “Look at this one!” she called to her brother.

Just before the end, Leo put the last book in his own bag. Imani looked at him. “You have to pay too,” she said. He laughed and put fifty pence in the box. They counted ten pounds. On Monday, they bought three small plants with the money and put them by the school door.

**Poppy.** Hi. Are these the only two books left at the school fair?

**Ali.** Yes. We came here to sell twenty books, and you’re our last customer. Are you looking for a story?

**Poppy.** Yes, but this book about space looks good too. What’s the price of a book?

**Ali.** They were a pound each this morning. See the new price on the sign? We made the change so more people could buy one. Each book costs fifty pence now.

**Poppy.** Oh, that’s cheap! I can buy both, then. How much altogether?

**Ali.** One pound, please. We’re going to buy plants for the garden with the money.

**Poppy.** I’ve got it here. Can I pay now and leave the books with you while I get my coat?

**Ali.** Yes. I’ll put them under the table with your name on them.

**Poppy.** Thanks. My sister will love the space book. She looks at the moon every night.

Nell and Jay wanted to have a picnic by the river. The weather was good when they left home. Nell brought egg sandwiches, and Jay carried a picnic blanket in his bag. It was only a short walk from their house to the river, so they left their coats at home.

At first there was just one grey cloud. By the time they got to the river, the sky was dark. They felt the first drops of rain just as Jay opened the bag. The grass was already wet. Little rings appeared on the river wherever the drops fell. Their sandwiches were still dry, but they needed to change the plan quickly.

Jay wanted to eat inside the café. It was full, and there was a queue outside the door. Then the two friends remembered that the library had wide steps under a roof. The library was closed on Sunday, but they could still sit on the steps. Nobody needed to go past them.

They put the blanket on the steps and sat down. From there, they could see the river and watch people hurry home. “We’re outside, and we’re dry,” said Nell. Jay took a bite of his sandwich. “Next Sunday,” he said, “the coats go in the bag first.”

**Sam.** The weather was so good this morning. Where did that big cloud come from?

**Jo.** I don’t know, but look at the rain now. I left the picnic blanket outside, and one corner is already wet.

**Sam.** Can you bring it inside? We could put it near the window while we have lunch.

**Jo.** There’s a dance class in there. Look, all the tables are against the wall.

**Sam.** Then let’s change the plan. The park shelter has a roof and two benches. We could have our picnic there.

**Jo.** Are the benches dry? The wind is blowing the rain this way.

**Sam.** The one at the back is dry. There’s room for our bags as well. I can bring the sandwiches and water.

**Jo.** I’ll get the blanket and carry it to the shelter. I'll fold the wet corner back so we can sit on the dry part.

**Sam.** I’ll meet you on the dry bench at the back, then. I hope you like cheese sandwiches!

Maya and Rowan went to a new swimming pool on Saturday. At the bus station, they bought a ticket each and looked at the town map on the wall. The pool was opposite the library, so Rowan said, “Let’s look for the library from the bus.” It was a short journey. Neither of them had been to that part of town before.

They checked the sign to make sure they had the right bus and sat at the front where they could see the road. They saw a supermarket and then a park. When the bus left the park, Maya began to look for the library. “I hope we don’t miss our stop,” she said. The driver heard her and told them to get off at the next stop. The bus could not go past the library that day because people were working on the road.

At the bus stop, Maya looked up the pool on her phone. They went straight to the bank, turned left at the corner and walked past a row of houses. Then they saw the library. The entrance to the pool was right opposite it, across the road. They still had ten minutes before their swimming lesson. Before they went in, Rowan marked the bank on her map. “That's where we turned. I'll remember it now.”

**Alex.** Excuse me. Is this the right bus for the swimming pool? I bought a return ticket at the station. My sister's got one too, but we can't find the pool on this map.

**Driver.** Yes. It’s a short journey. You need to get off at the next stop after the park.

**Alex.** Is the pool next to that bus stop?

**Driver.** It’s two streets away. Go straight from the stop to the bank. Turn left at the corner, then walk to the library.

**Alex.** Right, left at the bank. Is the pool behind the library?

**Driver.** No, it’s opposite the library, on the other side of the road. There’s a big blue sign above the door.

**Alex.** That helps. Could you tell me when we get there? I don’t want to miss our stop. My little sister is asleep, and I need to wake her up.

**Driver.** Of course. Look, there’s the park now. You can get ready to leave when we stop again.

**Alex.** Thanks. Can we use these tickets on the way back too?

**Driver.** Yes. Keep both tickets for the way back.
