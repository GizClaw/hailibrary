Yun and Han wanted to put on a short play in their building’s meeting room. They moved four chairs and made a small stage at one end. Yun tried to hang a blue cloth between two chairs, but it fell when Han walked past. Their aunt brought a clothes rail from the next room. Han held the cloth up while Yun tied the corners to the bar. That's how they managed to hang it high enough. Now their stage had a curtain. Han went behind it and called out, “Can anyone see my feet?”

## Questions

1. Why did Yun and Han move the chairs?
2. What did the blue cloth become?

## Answers

① They needed space for a stage. ② A curtain.

Han went up on the stage and tried his first line. Yun sat in a chair near the front. His voice sounded fine there. Then she tried a chair in the back row. She could see Han's lips moving, but his voice hardly reached her. “Wait until I sit down again,” she said. Han turned to face her, took a breath and spoke in a loud, clear voice. “That is loud enough,” Yun told him. She could hear every word from the last chair.

## Questions

1. Why did Yun sit at the back?
2. What did Han change so Yun could hear him?

## Answers

① To find out what the last row could hear. ② He spoke more loudly and faced Yun.

In the play, Han heard a knock at the door. Yun tried making the sound on the real door. The door opened and a man looked in. He wanted to know who had knocked. The man laughed when Yun showed him the little stage. They laughed too, and his laugh followed him out into the hall. Their aunt found a piece of wood and took it behind the curtain. Han said his line in a clear voice. She tapped the wood twice. This sound was just right. It was not as loud as the real door, but nobody came in.

## Questions

1. What sound did they need for the play?
2. How did their aunt make the knock for the play?

## Answers

① A knock at the door. ② She tapped a piece of wood behind the curtain.

Han took three big steps across the stage and missed his place. Yun wanted to draw a line on the floor. Their aunt shook her head. “How will you get the line off the floor?” Yun drew the shape of a shoe on a paper card instead. She taped it to the floor beside the place where Han needed to stop. Han tried the scene again. He took one step after another until he stood by the mark. Yun could see the shoe from the front row, so she tried a smaller mark. Han could still see it from the stage.

## Questions

1. Why did Yun make the mark smaller?
2. What did Yun draw to help him?

## Answers

① The big mark was easy to see from the front row. ② An outline of a shoe on a paper card.

Rui wanted to act, but she didn't want to stand alone in front of everyone. “Would you like the role of the cat?” Han asked. “It sleeps by the door.” Rui tried to act like a sleepy cat. That was a role she could play sitting down. She sat on a cushion behind the curtain. When Han took his last step, she moved two fingers like ears and made a tiny sound. Her voice was soft, but Yun heard her. “Leave the curtain open for that bit in the show,” said Rui. “I want them to see my ears.”

## Questions

1. Why did the small child like the new role?
2. How did she act like a cat without getting up?

## Answers

① She could play it sitting down. ② She moved two fingers like ears and made a tiny sound.

Han wanted to save a seat for Grandad. He put a paper card on a chair near the back wall. Grandad could see the stage from there, but he would have to step past several chairs. Yun tried the walk from the entrance to his chair. If the room was full, it would be hard to get to that seat. Han moved the card to the end of the front row, close to the entrance. Han tried the walk too. Grandad could go straight to his seat with his walking stick.

## Questions

1. Why was the first seat hard to reach?
2. Which seat did Han save in the end?

## Answers

① Grandad would have to squeeze past several chairs. ② The seat at the end of the front row, near the entrance.

Han sometimes forgot a line as he stepped out and saw people looking at him. Yun said she could prompt him by saying the first word very softly. Near the end of the play he forgot his next line. Yun started to give him the prompt. Before Han heard her, he saw Rui open two fingers like cat ears. It was his sign to talk to the cat. “Wake up, little cat!” His voice came out loud and clear. After the play he thanked Yun for waiting. “I nearly said it for you,” she admitted.

## Questions

1. What did Yun plan to do if Han forgot his line?
2. Which sign helped him instead?

## Answers

① Whisper the first word. ② Rui put up two fingers like cat ears.

When the curtain came down, their aunt helped the children take it off the bar. Yun and Han folded it together. Rui wanted to help fold the last bit of the curtain, so she held one blue corner until the others were ready. They put back the chairs and gave the cloth back to her. On the way out, Rui said she had an idea for the next play. The cat could have a bigger role. “It wakes up and walks all over you,” she told Han. She walked two fingers along his arm. “Can we try that together on Sunday?”

## Questions

1. Who helped take the curtain down?
2. What idea did Rui have for next time?

## Answers

① Their aunt helped the children. ② The cat could wake up and walk all over Han.
