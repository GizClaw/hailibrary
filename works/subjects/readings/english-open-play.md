Qiu’s group made a game in the school yard. Four players stood in four corners. Qiu stood in the middle and threw a soft ball. Anyone who could catch it got a point and threw it back. The team kept score on paper. After five turns, Qiu saw that Lei had caught the ball four times. Lei's corner had the most space, and Qiu kept choosing the easiest throw.

They changed the rule. The thrower could choose the next corner, but had to choose all four before starting another round. Soon everyone had a chance to catch it. At the end, instead of keeping a score for each person, the group counted how many catches the whole team could make in a row. This time the game lasted until the bell.

## Questions

1. Why did the first few turns seem unfair?
2. How did the new rule get everyone involved?

## Answers

① Qiu kept throwing to Lei's corner, so the others hardly got a turn. ② The thrower had to choose all four corners before the next round.

After rain, the group tried the game again. Near one corner, a ball hit the wet surface and slid past Qiu’s shoes. Lei wanted to wipe the ground and start at once, but the surface was slippery under their shoes. Qiu looked for dry ground where a thrown ball would be easier to catch. She found it in another part of the yard.

The team drew a small chalk circle for each player there. From one circle, Qiu rolled the ball to Lei. It did not slide past him this time. Then they tried a short, gentle throw. They waited until the rest of the yard was dry before anyone used the old corners again. On their plan of the yard, Lei drew a circle around the dry patch. Next time it rained they would know where to look first.

## Questions

1. Why did the ball slide in one corner?
2. What did the circles on the ground show?

## Answers

① There was water on the smooth ground. ② Where each player could stand on a dry surface.

Some players in Qiu’s game said the name of the person who should catch the ball. Others used their hands. Lei tried to make one signal for everyone. “Touch your arm when you're ready to catch,” he said. He tried it himself. Two players near him saw that movement, but the players at the far end missed it.

Qiu brought a blue card to use as the signal. At first the card was too low. Then Lei held it above his head for the next turn, and everyone could see. Before the ball came, Lei tucked the card into his pocket to leave both hands free. After the catch he passed it on. The next player held the card high, touched her pocket and tucked it away. Only then did Lei throw.

## Questions

1. Why did the first signal fail?
2. What made the card easier to see?

## Answers

① Players at the far end missed the small movement of his hand. ② Lei held it up above his head.

A younger child named An played with Qiu’s group. He could reach a ball that came near his chest, but he had trouble with the distance between two of the corners. His throws often fell short. Lei was about to move all the circles. Then he saw An make a good throw from the shorter side of the yard.

Qiu asked what change would allow An to join the rest of the game. Beside each circle they measured out a second circle, one step closer to the middle. Players could choose the distance that worked for them. The new rule allowed An to send the ball all the way to Qiu. She caught it and stepped back into her first circle. “Again!” An called. Before the bell the team had reached its highest score that morning.

## Questions

1. Why did An's throws often fall short?
2. What did the team allow players to do?

## Answers

① Some of the corners were too far apart for him. ② Move one step closer for their turn.

Two teams played Qiu’s game on Friday. The rule was one point for a catch with both feet inside the circle. Both teams saw one player catch the ball. One team also saw that his foot was outside the circle. The other team thought the catch should count as a point. Each group had a reason, but they could not agree on what had happened.

Lei asked a younger girl to judge one round. She could stand to the side and watch only the feet. “I'll watch his feet,” she said. “You watch the ball.” To make it fair, the teams took turns sending someone to judge. Before the next round they read the rule together. When the girl said that a point did not count, both teams knew why. “Fair enough,” said Lei. He rolled the ball back to the circle for the next throw.

## Questions

1. Why did the two teams disagree about a point?
2. How did the teams share the job of judging?

## Answers

① One team saw a foot outside the circle and the other did not. ② Someone from each team could take a turn as judge.

On Monday the older team scored quickly. They had practiced the long throw together on Sunday. The younger team had never tried it and missed chance after chance to score. The old rule gave each team an equal number of turns, but Qiu could see that equal turns did not make this round much fun.

She offered a different challenge. What if everyone tried to make three gentle throws in a row? Each team would have enough time to try, and a good catch would help the next person. Lei thought they could improve the game further if players on a team moved a little farther apart only after three good catches. For once, the younger team could set its own distance. Before the bell, both teams asked for another round of the challenge.

## Questions

1. Why did one team have more chances to score?
2. What new challenge did the team choose?

## Answers

① The older team had practiced the long throw together. The younger team hadn't. ② Start close, make three gentle catches in a row, then try a longer throw.

Qiu wanted each round to last one minute. She could not watch a clock and catch the ball at the same time. She missed a good throw while looking up at the clock on the school wall. Lei suggested that the person at the back could count to sixty. But Lei counted much faster than Qiu. At his pace, a round was over before she was ready.

The next round had no clock. Qiu counted turns instead. She gave every corner two turns, and then the group started again. One round was slow, and another went at a faster pace, but everyone got the same chances. Qiu decided they could count points only if both teams wanted to. She put the score sheet down. “Today I'd like to look at the ball when it comes my way.”

## Questions

1. Why did Qiu stop watching the clock?
2. How did she decide when a round was over?

## Answers

① She missed a throw while looking at it. ② She ended a round after every corner had two turns.

At the end of the week, two new children came to join the game. One looked at the circles and thought all the places were taken. Qiu asked Lei to be his partner. They used the space between rounds to practice a catch. The other child wanted to practice a throw before the round began. She asked Qiu to stand only one step away. After both children had practiced, they could join the team at the next turn.

Qiu found the page with the rules on it. She turned it over and wrote one line. Each new player gets a partner for the first round. When Qiu moved up a class, the others could continue the game. The boy who thought all the places were taken already knew who to invite. “My little sister loves catching,” he said.

## Questions

1. What did each new player get to practice?
2. What was Qiu’s new rule?

## Answers

① The boy practiced catching with Lei. The girl practiced a short throw with Qiu. ② Each new player gets a partner for the first round.
