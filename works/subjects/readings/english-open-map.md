Po wanted to send his cousin a map of their afternoon walk. Gran drew the river on a piece of paper and left a gap for him to finish. Po drew the bridge first. Then he drew a line for their walk. “Can we walk on water?” Gran asked. His line crossed the river beside the bridge. Po rubbed it out and drew the line over the bridge itself. He turned the map so its river went the same way as the one in front of them. He put a mark at each end of the bridge.

## Questions

1. What did Po draw first?
2. Why did he move the first line?

## Answers

① A bridge. ② It crossed the river beside the bridge, where there was no way across.

On the map Po could see two ways to walk from the bridge. One path went past a row of houses. The other path stayed near the river. Po chose the one by the river because it went through the shade. “Where does the shade start?” asked Gran. Po led her back to the fork by the bridge. Both paths looked the same for the first few steps. He added an arrow to his map there. It pointed towards the trees.

## Questions

1. Why did Po choose the path by the river?
2. Where did he add an arrow?

## Answers

① It went through the shade. ② At the fork by the bridge.

Po and Gran had to choose a place to wait for his cousin. Po chose the first bench he could see. Gran said that people might pass it without looking because it was just past a busy crossing. Po walked farther along the path and found a bench between two trees. A green sign near one tree was easy to see from the bridge. “Pass the green sign and look for us between the trees,” Po said, trying out the directions. Gran could follow those even without a map.

## Questions

1. What was wrong with Po’s first meeting place?
2. Which bench did they choose?

## Answers

① People coming from the busy crossing might walk right past it. ② The one between two trees.

Behind the bench was a small, quiet place beside the river. Po heard a bird call from one of the trees. He could hear the water while he waited for the bird to call again. Gran said that the road was near, but the sound here was very different. “Listen,” said Gran when the bird called again. Po drew a tiny bird above the trees on his map. He wrote one word beside it so nobody would mistake it for a bird that was always there. LISTEN, it said. Even the road was quiet. He wanted his cousin to stop and listen there.

## Questions

1. What could they hear behind the bench?
2. How did Po mark the quiet place?

## Answers

① A bird calling and the river. ② He drew a bird by the trees and wrote LISTEN beside it.

The next week there was a fence across part of the path. Two workers were repairing a hole in the path. Po stopped before the fence. It was red and white, but it did not tell him how to get to the bench. Gran pointed to a path that ran behind a row of houses. A sign by the fence pointed that way, well clear of the repair work. Po changed the map by drawing a second line around the fence. On the way back, he checked that the two lines met again at the trees.

## Questions

1. Why could they not use their first path?
2. How did Po mark the way around it?

## Answers

① Workers were repairing a part of the path. ② He drew a second line around the fence.

Po brought home the map to make a smaller copy. His cousin wanted it to fit in a pocket. Po folded a test page and saw that the fold hid part of the name of the bridge. On a fresh copy, he moved the name to the left of the fold line. The bridge stayed where it belonged. Both the name and the picture fit on the front of the folded map. Now his cousin could pull it from his pocket and read the name without opening it.

## Questions

1. Why did Po move the label for the bridge?
2. What could his cousin see while the map was folded?

## Answers

① The fold hid part of its name. ② The bridge and its name.

On Saturday, Po let his cousin lead the way with the pocket map. “Do I get a guide?” his cousin asked. “Only if you ask me to guide you,” said Po. He tucked his hands in his pockets. His cousin could follow the map up to the bridge. After the bridge he stopped. He did not know which path to take because the arrow could point to either one. Po asked his cousin to show him which way the arrow seemed to lead. They turned the map to match the path. Po drew a longer arrow along the branch towards the trees. This time his cousin set off without a word.

## Questions

1. How did Po test his map?
2. Why did his cousin get confused at the fork?

## Answers

① He let his cousin lead with the map. ② The arrow could point to either path.

Po and his cousin finished the walk at the bench. Po thought he had finished the map too, but his cousin had another idea. Halfway across the bridge they had seen the river bend round the trees. He wanted that spot on the map. Po had walked past it many times without stopping. His cousin added a small blue window to show the view. They agreed to visit the bridge again on a rainy day and see whether the view changed. “On our next visit, you can guide me,” Po said. He gave his cousin the pencil.

## Questions

1. What did the cousin add to the map?
2. Why did they want to visit again?

## Answers

① A little blue window marking the view from the bridge. ② They wanted to see how it looked in the rain.
