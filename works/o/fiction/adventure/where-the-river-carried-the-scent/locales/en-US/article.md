# Where the River Carried the Scent

At a gray dawn, Ana clips a red search harness onto Moss beside the busy river station. Jo says, “Eli surveyed the northern reed plots yesterday afternoon and never returned for the evening check-in.”

Jo points beyond the footbridge toward the north-bank plots, where Eli was last seen. Jo says, “Overnight rain has swollen the river, so every team must use bridges and high-bank paths.”

Ana offers Moss a clean reed sample case that carries Eli's familiar scent. Other searchers and their equipment stand back to avoid adding confusing odors near the starting point.

Moss samples the air, then pulls downstream along the high bank. Ana says, “He is following airborne scent, not a visible trail on the ground.”

Near a wide river bend, Moss suddenly stops, faces the moving water, and gives his trained alert. Jo says, “Then Eli must be somewhere downstream along this bank.”

Ana rewards Moss but keeps the team in place. Ana says, “Moss smelled Eli here. That tells us where the scent reached him, not where Eli is.”

Dev's lightweight ribbon pole leans diagonally from the opposite bank toward Moss and the river bend. Dev says, “A crosswind may be carrying scent over the river before the curved banks turn it downstream.”

From a dry, safe overlook, they compare tall reeds, steep banks, moving water, and wind. The broad river bend could redirect both air and scent without showing where Eli actually walked.

The team crosses the footbridge, and Moss alerts again among south-bank reeds. Ana says, “We found scent on both banks. Dev's crosswind idea now fits better than the downstream guess.”

Jo builds two simple clay models: one shows Eli crossing; the other shows only airborne scent crossing. Jo says, “Both explanations still fit. We have to search them both.”

Separate search teams take the bridge and marked high-bank routes while avoiding the fast, swollen water. One checks every safe access point; another searches sheltered ground farther downstream.

Dev marks each alert with a colored pebble and records the ribbon direction. Dev says, “Mark the wind with every alert. It may shift before the next one.”

Light rain arrives, cooling the air, and the wet ribbon turns toward a new direction. An alert made under these conditions cannot be interpreted with the earlier wind alone.

Moss enters dense reeds, searches carefully, then loses the scent and looks back. Ana says, “He lost the scent. Give him room; we do not get to choose what he finds.”

Ana gives Moss water and examines his paws while he rests on a dry mat. Jo says, “Let him rest. Tired searchers miss things.”

A second canine team independently examines another high-bank sector beyond the reeds. Its separate search reduces the risk of treating Moss's path as the only possible path toward Eli.

After resting, Moss alerts near the mouth of a narrow side channel. A line of willows shelters the air above it.

Ana and Dev compare the new alert with their model and ribbon records. Dev says, “Both winds could carry scent toward this side channel.”

Jo stops and looks past the willow line. Jo says, “There is an old flood shelter past those willows. The high-bank path reaches it.”

The team follows that path and finds Eli waving from the dry stone doorway. Eli says, “I heard you, but the river was too loud for you to hear me.”

Eli shows a broken boot strap and explains why he stayed inside. Eli says, “Crossing wet ground with an unstable boot seemed riskier than waiting in shelter.”

Dev arranges alert pebbles, wind directions, and search sectors around the model. Ana says, “The alerts, wind, and safe routes brought us here together. None of them did it alone.”

Moss receives water and quiet praise as both canine teams reunite. Jo says, “I treated the first alert like an address. It was not.”

At sunset, the whole team returns along the high path above the fast river. Ana says, “Easy, Moss. We are going home by the high path.”
