# The City That Moved at Dawn

At dawn, rounded city blocks begin gliding quietly along broad ground rails. Gardens, homes, and public rooms can trade places as needs and seasons change.

Sen watches a physical model of the moving city. “Node should reduce energy use while keeping every route open,” she tells Lu.

Node projects several colored paths across the model and reports, “Lowest-energy arrangement selected.”

A home block enters cool morning shade while the public garden receives warm sunlight for its plants. Sen says, “The first exchange works.”

The city completes its early moves in an orderly pattern. Sen records the energy lights and smiles at the calm streets below.

Then the crowded market begins sliding away from its plaza. Lu turns to Sen. “Nobody here asked the market to leave.”

Vendors steady fruit, tools, and heavy baskets while customers wait on a safe platform. The movement causes no crash, yet trade and daily work stop.

“Your system counted power, but not the people carrying goods,” Lu says. Sen looks at the quiet rail gate. “I treated an open rail as permission to move.”

Node displays a short route and a longer curved route. “Short route uses less power,” it reports.

Sen points to the model's lower energy lights. Lu points to workers carrying goods farther from the transport stop.

An older vendor rests beside a heavy basket. Lu points toward her. “Node saved some power. She must carry that basket much farther.”

Sen closes the automatic route control. “Until we agree on better rules, Node will not move another block.”

Residents, vendors, and neighbors arrange colored block models around a wide table. They compare shade, walking distance, deliveries, public transport, and energy together.

A child places the market model beside the busy transport stop. Vendors add a wide unloading space for carts and baskets.

Lu places a bright consent token beside one proposed route. “Before the market moves, vendors must know where it goes and agree. They must also be able to stop it.”

Sen adds a large tactile pause button to Node. “Put this where anyone beside the rail can stop the move.”

Node waits with an amber light while people discuss the next move. At last it reports, “No shared approval detected. Holding position.”

The market finally takes the longer curved route. It stays beside transport and uses slightly more energy.

Homes settle into shade, gardens face the sun, and the market stays beside transport. People cross the connected paths while deliveries continue.

At the next dawn, the market stays still. Node waits beside Sen and Lu. Sen asks, “Shall we call the vendors?” Lu opens the route model. “Yes. We ask them before anything moves.”
