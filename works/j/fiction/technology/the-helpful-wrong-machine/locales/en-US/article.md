# The Helpful Wrong Machine

Em unveils Pebble at the market return counter. “It can sort every object in a blink.”

Pebble sorts round oranges into the produce tray and announces, “Round and orange. Match found.”

Then Pebble drops a red toy ball beside the oranges. “That is one very bouncy fruit,” Em jokes.

Em laughs and removes the ball. She expects the next choice to be better.

Pebble sends a smooth green soap bar toward the vegetables. “Green and smooth. Match found.”

Tala stops the belt before the soap reaches the food. “A wrong match can make a real mess.”

Em checks Pebble’s sensors. “It notices color and shape, but not use or material.”

Tala holds up a cup, a planter, and a broken lampshade. “These shapes look alike, but they belong in different places.”

Pebble pauses between the three objects. “One shape. Three possible groups.”

Em adds trays with different openings and surfaces. “Now material and use matter too.”

Tala calls the market workers over. “Bring the objects that are hardest to sort.”

A child offers a wooden toy shaped like fruit. Pebble scans it. “Its shape says food. Its material says toy.”

Em adds a coral paddle that pauses the belt. “Anyone can stop a doubtful choice.”

Pebble sends ordinary items onward. It places uncertain objects on a review table.

Tala reaches toward the review table, but it sits too high beside her cane. “I cannot reach that pile,” she tells Em.

Em lowers the table and clears a wide path around it. “I tested Pebble, but I forgot this work area.”

Workers examine the unusual objects together. Their explanations help Em improve the rules.

By evening, the food is safe and the review pile is small. Pebble sets down one strange object. “Human review, please.”
