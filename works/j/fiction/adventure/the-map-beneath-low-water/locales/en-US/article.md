# The Map Beneath Low Water

Ka reaches a coastal inlet with a tube of maps. He must collect seeds from an island greenhouse.

Ka traces the straight crossing. “The shortest line should be the quickest way,” he says.

The greenhouse glows across shallow water. No bridge joins the island to the dock.

As the tide falls, curved wet sandbars appear. They do not match Ka's straight line.

“The map still points straight across,” Ka insists, though he stays safely on the dock.

Mi arrives in a small ferry and studies the inlet. “A line on paper cannot hold the water still,” she says.

Mi points to wet seaweed high on a dock post. “The water reached that mark earlier.”

Ka compares the seaweed mark, current waterline, and new sandbar. “The inlet changes while my map stays the same,” he admits.

A narrow channel moves quickly between two sandbars. “Exposed sand is not a safe road,” Mi warns.

Ka rolls up the fixed-route map. “I need today’s water clues.”

Mi brings Ka into the ferry from the dock.

Mi follows a curved route through the open channel. The ferry stays in moving water.

A floating reed marker leans with the current. Ka watches Mi steer against it.

They reach the greenhouse and collect a sealed seed chest.

On the return trip, rising water covers the sandbar edges. “This place needs another picture now,” Ka says.

Ka sketches three water shapes on separate blank cards. Each card shows a different tide stage.

Mi arranges the cards beside a model ferry. “Compare the water and markers before you choose.”

At sunset, Ka and Mi watch the inlet change again. Ka rolls the three cards together. “Tomorrow, I will check the water before I choose one.”
