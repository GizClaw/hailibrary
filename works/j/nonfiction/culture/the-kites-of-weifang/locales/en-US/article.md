# The Kites of Weifang

On a spring morning, Xiaoman enters Master Gao's kite workshop in Weifang. She hopes to make a kite for the city's international festival.

Finished kites fill the room, but Master Gao begins with two thin pieces of bamboo. Xiaoman sees a swallow kite with no color. “Can mine be a swallow?” she asks. He nods and places the pieces across each other.

Master Gao shapes the bamboo with care and ties it into a light frame. Xiaoman holds one end steady while he works on the other.

The two wings should match, yet one bamboo end sticks out. “Let's check it now,” Xiaoman says. “The paper may hide the small difference.”

She lifts the frame from the middle. One side falls lower. Master Gao cuts a little from the long end, and the next lift looks even.

Some Weifang kites use paper over bamboo. Others use silk. Xiaoman chooses plain paper that feels light in her hands.

Master Gao spreads paste along the bamboo. Xiaoman presses the paper from the center toward each wing, so the cover lies flat.

When the paper dries, Xiaoman paints red wings and blue and gold feathers on the swallow. Master Gao mixes paint in plain bowls while she works.

Next Master Gao ties a short string to the frame. He ties the flying line to it with a knot. Moving the knot can change how the kite flies.

Xiaoman lifts the kite by the knot to test its balance. The left wing hangs low. “I finished painting, but we aren't done checking,” she says.

Master Gao moves the knot a little. Xiaoman lifts the kite again, and both wings stay level. Only a field test can show what it does in wind.

They try a low launch in a clear field away from power lines. The kite pulls to one side, so they stop and make one more small change. The next short flight is steadier.

On festival day, people come from China and many other countries. Xiaoman sees long dragons, bright birds, and new shapes, each made to meet the wind.

Her swallow rises above the field without turning over. Xiaoman watches both wings stay level and feels the line pull steadily in her hands.
