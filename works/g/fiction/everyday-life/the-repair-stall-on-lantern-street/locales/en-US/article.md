# The Repair Stall on Lantern Street

Auntie Chen brought a small radio to Grandpa Lu’s stall. “It went quiet,” she said.

Grandpa Lu stepped just inside for his tool tray. He could still see Mei. She leaned close, eager to fix the radio herself.

Mei opened the battery door. One battery wobbled when she touched it, but she hurried on.

She wiped the battery ends and turned the round knob. The radio stayed quiet.

Over the past few weeks, Amara had learned a little Chinese. Mei’s dog Bean trotted over as Amara arrived. “I can fix it,” Mei said, turning the knob. Still nothing.

Amara crouched and pointed at the battery door. “What did you try first?” she asked. Mei remembered the little wobble.

That wobble was a clue. They removed both batteries before looking inside the empty case.

Two metal springs sat at the ends. One stood tall, but the other sat low. The low spring could not press firmly against the battery. The radio got no power.

Grandpa Lu returned. Mei showed him the bent spring. “May I lift it?” she asked.

He nodded and held the radio steady. Mei used his small pliers to lift the spring. Amara watched.

They put the batteries back the same way. Mei turned the knob. Warm music filled the stall.

Auntie Chen smiled when Mei returned the radio. Bean tilted his head at the music, and Amara laughed.
