# The Cloud Diary

On Monday morning, Lili brought her orange notebook to Grandma Ruan's old weather garden. She planned to watch the sky there for seven days. Her younger cousin Yan arrived in his blue raincoat and announced, “Dark clouds mean rain. I can predict the whole week.” Grandma Ruan said, “Then record what happens after each guess.”

Above the levee, separate white clouds had rounded tops and flat, gray bases. While Lili checked Grandma Ruan's cloud guide, Yan studied their darker parts. “These are cumulus clouds,” she said. Yan pointed to one gray base and predicted rain before lunch.

Although the clouds crossed the town, they never grew taller, and the rice-field paths were still dry by evening. Lili wrote the result beside Yan's guess. Grandma Ruan asked, “Was the base dark?” “Yes,” Yan said. “Did it rain?” “No,” he answered, folding down his unused hood.

On Tuesday, thin white threads curved high above the tiled roofs. Lili said, “These are cirrus clouds, not the low rounded clouds from yesterday.” Such high, thin clouds can be an early clue that weather is changing, but they don't promise rain. Yan searched for a dark base, found none, and kept watching.

As the white threads spread farther across the sky, no rain reached the garden that day. Lili recorded their shape, height, and the dry path so she could compare them with what came next. Yan said, “I won't write the ending before it happens.”

By Wednesday, the high thin clouds had given way to a gray sheet spreading across the sky. It thickened, hid the sun, and seemed to lower toward the hills. Lili said, “I'll leave the cloud name blank for now,” while recording its shape, coverage, and the time.

Before noon, fine rain crossed the river and kept falling. In the guide, nimbostratus was shown as a thick, broad layer linked with steady rain. The picture and words matched the sky. Yan said, “This dark cloud did bring rain.” Grandma Ruan asked, “How was it different from Monday's clouds?”

Yan traced the change through the diary. “First the high, thin clouds spread. Then the sheet grew thicker, looked lower, and hid the sun before steady rain began.” Lili marked the sequence as a useful clue, not a promise. By Thursday morning, the layer had broken into patches, and sunlight reached the wet fields.

After lunch on Thursday, small cumulus clouds appeared again. At first, they looked much like Monday's clouds. Lili drew one cloud's low, rounded outline, then closed the notebook until the next check.

Between two checks, that cloud grew much taller as its bright top rose in large folds and its base widened. Lili drew the new outline beside the first. Yan didn't predict rain right away. “It's getting taller fast. Maybe it'll bring a shower,” he said.

A low roll of thunder came from beyond the river. Grandma Ruan did not wait for rain. “Inside, both of you,” she said. She led Lili and Yan away from the open garden and kept them back from the windows.

From deep inside the room, they saw the tall cloud spread a flat top. Lili checked the guide. “The tower has become a cumulonimbus cloud,” she said. A heavy shower fell on the garden for a short time, then stopped.

On Friday, puddles shone beside the weather instruments. Lili placed Monday's cumulus sketch beside Thursday's tower. Both clouds had dark bases, but only one had grown quickly and brought a heavy shower. Yan pointed to the tower sketch. “It grew so much taller,” he said.

Saturday's sky held high cirrus clouds and small cumulus clouds. After Yan compared them with the earlier pages, he said, “I can't tell yet if it'll rain.” They carried coats because the weather could still change. As they walked, the high clouds stayed thin, the low clouds stayed small, and the afternoon remained dry.

On Sunday, a row of cumulus clouds appeared over the warm fields. Yan made one last prediction. “Maybe there will be a shower if those clouds keep growing.” Lili said, “Let's wait and see. We can check the official weather forecast too.”

By sunset, the clouds had stopped growing, and no rain had fallen. After Lili wrote the week's last result and closed the diary, Yan studied one cloud with a gray base. Instead of announcing rain, he asked, “How is it changing?”
