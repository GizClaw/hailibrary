# The Garden on the Long Orbit

With thirty-seven light cycles left before resupply, the cargo carrier docked beside the garden habitat. Water trembled on the tips of lettuce leaves whenever a transfer cart crossed the neighboring hull. Every status light remained green.

“All monitored values are still inside their limits,” Seed reported. Yuan opened the last four thermal records. “Then say that. Do not call the whole mission nominal while our margin falls four cycles in a row.” Earlier flights still mattered, but none had tested the garden in this exact docked shape.

Yuan began each shift at the seed cabinet rather than the fastest-growing vines. Twelve crop zones held different ages and varieties, but replacement seedlings shared one lighting schedule and every reserve batch occupied the same environmental cabinet.

Ivo watched through the garden window. “Food reserves can cover a smaller harvest. They cannot replace every seedling if one cabinet fails.” Fresh food, water recovery, and crew routine still mattered. The team marked the common cabinet as the first risk that could outlast the voyage.

Heat entered the garden through lamps, pumps, electronics, crew activity, and absorbed external energy. Cooling loops carried much of it toward exterior radiators, whose surfaces released thermal energy toward colder surroundings. Changes in wall, water, and growing-medium temperature also revealed energy being stored or released.

Aro traced the path on a clear panel and left two terms unfinished. The garden averaged eighty-four shipboard thermal units per interval, with a normal range of six. Pump and crew contributions still came from subsystem logs rather than measurements beside the plants. “Leave those spaces open,” he said. “Estimated is not measured.”

Ivo's carrier had docked beside the garden to shorten protected cargo transfer, but its warm hull now occupied part of the angular field that radiator wing R2 once presented to deep space. Ivo pointed to the gap. “The carrier never touches the radiator, and our clearance meets the rule. How can empty space count as a blockage?” Aro answered in terms of radiation, not contact: geometry determined which surfaces exchanged energy, so a separated object could replace part of a cold view with a warm one and reduce effective rejection.

Across four matched light cycles, minimum thermal margin fell from eighteen units to eleven, then eight and six. Measurement uncertainty placed the latest value somewhere between four and eight. The temperatures were legal; the room left for another disturbance was becoming small.

Ivo dragged the docking log beside the trend. “Four cycles are not a verdict. Docking, lamp changes, sensor offset, or a slow controller could all draw this line.” Aro moved the cargo obstruction from the conclusion column back among the possible causes. The trend demanded action, but it did not choose the explanation.

Leaf edges curled inward in the upper part of zone G7. Yuan checked moisture, airflow, root-zone conductivity, and lamp history. G8 shared the same water branch and looked unchanged, while G7 had recently received a replacement lamp driver.

She handed the damaged leaf to Aro. “Heat is one possibility. So are the lamp, water, driver, and plant age. Find out when this began, where it appears, and whether the leaves recover.” The electrical log soon revealed an intermittent rise in the new driver's power use.

Aro found a stronger chronology in the cooling loop. Three sensors along the return path rose after docking, and the radiator inlet-to-outlet difference moved in the same direction; the turn fell inside one logged configuration interval. Seed called the association strong for such a short record, but not fully independent, because the sensors shared calibration, acquisition, coolant flow, and environmental inputs. Ivo followed the limitation to its practical question: “Could a change in the reference electronics make all three drift together?”

Aro divided the model into four linked parts: sources added heat, loops moved it, structures and contents stored it, and exterior surfaces rejected it. Sensors watched only a few points along that route. The team kept four explanations alive—blocked cold view, rising internal load, shared measurement bias, and slow control response.

Each explanation had to predict a different pattern before the next test. Geometry should keep R2 warmer than comparable radiator sectors. Load should move the whole margin with electrical demand. A shared bias should disagree with portable references. Yuan removed the phrase most likely from the panel. “Write down what would make us abandon each one.”

Three options emerged. Ivo acknowledged that undocking would clear the radiator view and separate failure paths, but it would delay medicine, repair parts, and water equipment while consuming maneuver reserve. Aro noted that staying docked preserved rapid transfer yet maintained uncertain thermal coupling. Yuan refused the apparent binary. “We can reconfigure locally: change attitude in stages, reduce controllable heat, alter transfer periods, and test a shade that never obstructs radiator emission.”

The table for configurations A, B, and C filled with thermal margin, power, crop darkness, maneuver reserve, and cargo delay. Someone began assigning points. Ivo covered the total with one hand. “Late emergency medicine and one less basket of lettuce do not belong in the same sum.”

They cleared the score and named the constraints that could not be traded away: safety lighting, medical access, essential power, and crop dark-period limits. Everything else would remain visible as a separate consequence.

Family A preserved crop lighting and immediate cargo access. Under median assumptions, Aro calculated a minimum margin of only three units by cycle twelve; with unfavorable view and load together, the predicted interval crossed the limit by cycle nine. Seed stressed that the crossing was a model outcome, not a scheduled failure, although it placed unsafe states among plausible conditions before resupply. Ivo kept one genuine advantage on the table: A required no maneuver, transfer interruption, or new hardware during the next maintenance window.

Family B would undock and turn sharply toward a favorable thermal attitude. Yuan traced both sides: modeled heat rejection improved, but crop illumination shifted, solar generation fell during two phases, and cargo access paused. Seed's median margin rose to twenty-one units, though the interval stayed wide because the attitude had ground-test support and little flight evidence for this assembled convoy. Ivo stated the constraint the thermal model could not erase. “Water-treatment spares can move early. One medicine package cannot leave controlled storage before its clinical window.”

Family C did not promise one decisive move. Seed explained that it began with load reduction and staggered lighting, then allowed small attitude changes only after measured gates were satisfied. Uncertainty widened after cycle fifteen because this exact phased policy had never operated end to end. Aro made the first gate concrete: conservative margin above eight units, stable coolant flow, unchanged safety lighting, and no crop zone beyond its dark-period limit. Yuan set the later gate at two degrees, conditional on R2 matching the geometry model and solar power remaining above reserve.

The first intervention would last only two complete light cycles. Corridor lighting dropped to its certified safety level, high-power work moved to separate periods, and selected crop lamps alternated while comparison zones kept their usual schedule.

Before anyone touched a control, Aro recorded the stop conditions: margin below seven conservative units, abnormal coolant flow, lost safety lighting, excessive crop darkness, or any control fault. Yuan sealed the predicted signatures for each model. “If we rewrite the prediction after the result, we have tested nothing.”

Forty minutes into the test, Ivo found both maintenance teams assigned to the same rest period. The next shift would have no qualified pump inspector. She pulled the schedules apart. “The total load cannot rise, and both teams cannot rest at once.”

In the dimmer corridor, one technician opened the wrong tool locker and switched on a task lamp. The revised log separated commanded load, measured load, deferred work, and staffing effects instead of hiding them inside one successful thermal number.

Yuan paired crop zones by variety, age, irrigation branch, and earlier growth. One member of each pair changed schedule while the other kept its baseline. The plants were not duplicates; old damage and different positions still mattered.

The lighting command removed fourteen thermal units, but extra pumping added two back. Aro changed the net reduction to twelve. Yuan tapped the missing pair of units. “Record those separately. The command did not remove fourteen from the habitat.”

After net load fell, coolant temperature stabilized and conservative margin stopped shrinking. Under comparable load, R2 remained warmer than the other radiator sectors. Portable references agreed with the fixed sensors closely enough to make a shared bias less likely, though not impossible.

Yuan read the overlaid traces twice. “Lower load stopped the margin loss, but R2 is still warm. We have to manage both in the next phase.” The simple load-only and geometry-only accounts gave way to a model in which both pathways mattered.

After two light cycles, every crop zone remained within its declared short-term range. Two varieties expanded leaves more slowly, but the brief test could not establish final yield, nutrition, disease response, or performance during the rest of the voyage.

Ivo placed the revised staffing record beside the plant results. “Rest periods are compliant now, but these two repairs are late. Put the delay into the trial record.” Yuan left both cells at full contrast.

The docking dispute exposed another weakness. Medicine, scrubber cartridges, water-pump parts, and seed-cabinet spares all sat inside Ivo's carrier because one hatch made routine transfer fast. Closing that hatch could interrupt several essential repairs at once.

Aro turned two identical pump packages over in his hands. “A second copy does not help if both copies share this bay, this power bus, or this hatch.” The crew stopped asking how many spares they owned and began asking which working set remained reachable after each isolation.

The team divided the remaining voyage into six operational phases, each with its own lighting, attitude, cargo window, maintenance, and review plan. Ivo moved only the parts required by near-term work and credible emergencies. Emptying the carrier would clog the passage and make the right adapter harder to find.

The first water-pump case locked into the floor of a habitat. Even with the cargo bay isolated, its repair crew could now reach a complete set that fitted the local loop.

The convoy turned by two degrees. Blue planetary light shifted along the window frame while solar power, coolant flow, and internal load stayed inside their gates. R2 responded within the interval predicted by the geometry model; other sectors changed less.

Yuan deleted proved from the record template. “R2 moved when we changed its view, but we changed it by only two degrees. Do not approve a larger turn from this result.”

Crop lighting shifted by variety and growth stage. The median projection placed edible yield fourteen percent below the original peak plan, with a range from nine to twenty-one percent. Stored food could cover the calories.

Ivo looked toward a herb planter that crews visited during every rest period. “Reserves can replace the calories. They cannot replace what people pick from that pot.” Its peppery scent once crossed half the dining corridor. Yuan added fresh-food variety to the next review.

Aro designed a shade that reduced absorbed energy on the carrier-facing structure without entering the radiator's view or motion path. Contamination, surface aging, seams, and angle still limited the model, so the shade remained a supplement rather than a replacement for heat rejection.

Ivo held the deployment model halfway open. “This is the position that worries me.” The panel was redesigned to latch clear when unpowered and to retract from inside the habitat. No crew member would have to leave the hull to rescue it.

The first phased harvest was smaller than planned, and the leaves remained healthy by the measurements already taken. One harvest could not settle questions about flavor, water recovery, labor, nutrition, or the long-term condition of replacement seedlings.

Seed reminded the team that every batch still shared convoy pressure, core power, navigation, and human maintenance. Yuan steadied a tray drifting in the weak gravity. “I know.” She amended the resilience claim so that it applied only to failures the team had actually analyzed.

Critical cargo was divided among three habitats by function, storage need, access time, and common failure exposure. Each habitat kept a local inventory copy, and paper location cards remained available if the central service failed.

During an isolation drill, two crews retrieved complete water-loop repair sets. A wrong adapter label delayed the third by twelve minutes. Ivo peeled it away. “The hatch opened. The label sent them to the wrong fitting.” They replaced the label and kept the delay in the handover record.

Seed reported three results separately: median peak edible yield was fourteen percent lower, conservative thermal margin stayed above nine units over the validated horizon, and essential supplies remained recoverable after the tested single-node isolation. Farther ahead, crop development, surface aging, maneuvers, maintenance, and compound failures widened the uncertainty.

Yuan turned off the green success arrows. Three curves remained on the display, each opening into a broader band at its far end.

At resupply, the convoy handed over vegetables, viable seedlings, thermal records, configuration histories, failed checks, and every model version that had guided a decision. Aro stopped a technician who was copying the thermal thresholds. “Do not carry those numbers into another vehicle. Its shape, load, and maintenance conditions will be different.”

The method could travel: trace where heat entered and left, make competing explanations face the same evidence, declare stop conditions before changing the system, and keep watching consequences outside the subsystem that first raised the alarm.

The garden docked with the carrier again. Beside R2, the new shade remained locked in its clear position. Yuan recorded the tested configuration, the uncertainty that widened beyond it, and the conditions for the next measurement.

“Next phase acceptable,” Seed said. “This judgment covers the current configuration and the tested single-node failures. It does not cover unobserved combinations.” Outside the window, the blue planet had narrowed to a bright disk. Ivo fastened the last distributed cargo case. One row of garden lamps went dark; another came on farther down the habitat.
