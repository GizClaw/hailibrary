1. Compared headings and every paragraph boundary: one title, no added chapter headings, all 30 source paragraphs represented in order.
2. Walked every paragraph for clauses, images, dialogue, numbers and causal links. Corrected the final livestock list to sheep and goats, and retained Euryclea's two hands covering the speaker's mouth.
3. Read dialogue by character and checked speaker, listener and intent: the nurse's announcement, Penelope's doubt and test, the son's reproach, the husband's anger, recognition and future instructions remain distinct.
4. Scanned the complete translation against the translationese reference. Replaced an ambiguous term for Eurynome's bedroom duties so it does not imply a sexual role.
5. Checked shared names and recurring images: the olive-root bed, the iron heart, castaways seeing land, oars as wings, and the remaining voyage. Preserved the prophecy's death coming from the sea.
6. Read the complete Chinese aloud, including the long account of past voyages, for rhythm, intelligibility and the changing mood of the reunion.
7. Checked fidelity of tone: violence, sexuality, Helen's blame and the proposed seizure of livestock remain as in the source, without a new moral or a modern published translation.
