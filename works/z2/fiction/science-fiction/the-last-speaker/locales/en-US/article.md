# The Last Speaker

The ferry reached Pelu Island beneath a sky the color of wet slate. Mara Venn kept her recorder inside its case until Ilyan Sero met her at the empty pier. Twelve years earlier, he had corrected her first Tava greeting and then made her ask permission properly. Now he was eighty-seven. The clinic had warned that his lungs were failing, and the public language laboratory wanted one final month of sessions. “You brought the patient machine,” Ilyan said, noticing Oren's amber equipment behind her. Mara answered, “I brought a machine that has not earned its claims.” He smiled. “Then it may be ready to learn.”

News reports called Ilyan “the last speaker,” a phrase precise enough for a headline and unstable everywhere else. No other islander could sustain the range of Tava that he used for boatbuilding, weather, kinship, argument, and ceremonial narrative. Yet Seli understood most of her grandfather's speech. Two fishers knew reef terms he had forgotten. An aunt remembered mourning verses but refused to perform them for strangers. Mara's survey therefore used a narrower statement: Ilyan was the last person whom the local language council currently recognized as fully fluent across customary domains. Even that description depended on who asked, what counted as a domain, and whether a private competence could be tested ethically.

Seli met them at the weather station, which the council had lent to the project. She had labeled no walls; colored cords and object shapes organized the room without written Tava. “If I answer slowly, outsiders say I don't speak,” she told Mara. “If Grandfather supplies a word, they say the answer was his.” Ilyan replied in Tava, too quickly for Oren, and Seli laughed before rendering the sense: “He says a net with one repaired knot is still a net, unless the hole is where the fish goes.” Mara heard both affection and a methodological warning. A single scale could conceal different strengths, and the act of testing could alter the performance it claimed to observe.

Mara's archive already held nine hundred hours of audio and video, transcriptions, translations, grammatical notes, and contextual records. Together those materials formed a corpus: a structured body of language evidence rather than a disembodied list of sentences. Some sessions showed Ilyan shaping planks while naming their grain. Others followed disputes over fishing boundaries or quiet conversations after funerals. Each file recorded who was present, why the event occurred, and what access the speakers had permitted. Still, the collection was uneven. Childhood games filled many afternoons; intimate disagreements were scarce. The archive reflected not Tava in its entirety, but what particular people had chosen and been able to record.

Ilyan refused to begin in the station. He led them to a boat shed where his cousin Naro was repairing a sail. For forty minutes, the two men debated whether a seam would hold when the wind shifted. Their hands interrupted their sentences; shared glances replaced names; a joke depended on an earlier failed voyage. Mara's microphones captured overlapping voices, hammer taps, and the long pause before Naro conceded. Oren worried about noise. Ilyan tapped the recorder. “The noise tells you why I stopped speaking.” Back at the station, Mara annotated the tools, gestures, participants, and prior event. Sound without those relations would preserve forms while erasing much of what the forms were doing.

The next session used picture cards and small objects to elicit contrasts that ordinary conversation might never supply efficiently. Mara moved a shell uphill, downhill, toward the sea, and away from it. Ilyan changed the verb each time, then rejected one scene because the imagined path crossed a cliff. Elicitation revealed a pattern; it also manufactured a peculiar world in which shells traveled on command. Mara asked why he had chosen a rare ending. “Because you showed me, not because someone told me,” he said. The answer linked grammar to evidence available in the moment. Oren's model would receive the sentence and metadata, but it would not inhabit the room from which Ilyan had selected it.

Oren unfolded the laboratory device. Its speech model had learned statistical patterns from council-approved portions of the archive. Given a prompt in the common language, it could propose Tava audio; given Tava audio, it could rank possible transcriptions. Oren avoided saying that it understood. The laboratory's public announcement had been less disciplined: PRESERVATION COMPLETE, it had declared before the council forced the message offline. No one on Pelu had approved that phrase. “A model can be useful without being the language,” Oren said. Seli asked who would decide useful for what. He began listing benchmark scores, stopped, and closed the display. “You should,” he answered. “But I have not yet built that decision into the system.”

Mara proposed three separate claims. First, the archive could preserve documented performances and analyses. Second, the model might reproduce sounds and extend observed patterns within measured limits. Third, neither result alone demonstrated continuing social use or transmission. Ilyan listened, then asked why the laboratory had compressed those claims into one triumphant verb. Oren admitted that funders understood “preserved” more readily than “a governed collection with incomplete coverage.” Ilyan turned the carved tide bird in his palm. “Your short word purchased attention by spending our uncertainty.” No one converted the sentence into a slogan. Mara wrote it in her field notes because it identified a real transaction: institutional clarity had been gained by concealing the project's disputed boundary.

Oren's first evaluation resembled a sealed examination. He withheld recorded sentences from training, asked the model to reconstruct their sound patterns, and compared its outputs with the originals. On common words and familiar speakers, the results were strong. Breathier vowels and rapid overlaps produced more errors. The model's smooth synthetic voice disguised those failures because listeners heard confidence where the scoring program saw distance. Ilyan requested the uncorrected outputs. One misplaced consonant changed “reef channel” into a harmless nonword; another changed a kin term into a different branch of the family. The average score recorded both as errors of similar size. For a speaker choosing whom to address, their social consequences were not similar at all.

Mara accepted the acoustic test and rejected the conclusion Oren's report attached to it. Reconstructing hidden recordings measured whether the model generalized beyond its exact training examples. It did not establish that the system could enter a new conversation, identify what participants knew, or repair a misunderstanding. Oren revised the report while they watched. “Accurate speech-model reconstruction on selected archival data,” he read aloud. “No validated claim of independent fluency.” Seli approved the second sentence but circled “selected.” Which speakers, genres, and recording conditions had been selected would determine what the number meant. The test was not false; its frame had been missing. Once restored, the result became less spectacular and more informative.

For the first community test, Ilyan placed his carved tide bird inside a cloth bag while Seli waited outside. Tava verbs required speakers to distinguish, in certain contexts, what they had witnessed from what they knew by report. Mara told the model, in the common language, that the bird was hidden. Seli then entered and asked where it was. The model produced a fluent answer with the ending frequent in recordings about carved birds. Ilyan frowned. That ending claimed direct perception. The machine had received a description, not seen the hiding. Its sentence fitted archival neighbors but contradicted the route by which its information had arrived. Oren marked the output wrong, although every sound was immaculate.

Oren traced the failure. Most archived mentions of carved birds came from workshops in which speakers handled finished objects, so the witnessed ending dominated nearby patterns. The metadata described cameras, participants, and activities, but the model had not been designed to reason over those records. When Oren added an explicit source tag, the next answer used the reported ending. Ilyan did not celebrate. “Now it obeys your tag,” he said. “Will it know when your tag is wrong?” The repair demonstrated that engineering could narrow one failure under stated conditions. It also created a new dependency on whoever labeled the prompt. Mara added label provenance and uncertainty to the proposed evaluation, rather than calling the contradiction solved.

The second test concerned a tide story that children once completed collectively. Ilyan narrated until the youngest sailor ignored a warning and entered a luminous fog. The model had to continue. It generated a polished rescue: the sailor followed singing birds home. Ilyan shook his head, not because Tava forbade the words, but because the tale's conventional next turn required listeners to challenge the sailor's account before any rescue could occur. Several endings were possible after that exchange. The model had produced a probable adventure ending from translated stories in its broader system, bypassing an obligation visible only across Tava performances. Its grammar survived. The event in which that grammar belonged had been replaced.

Seli supplied the challenge line, and Ilyan continued with an ending Mara had never recorded. The sailor admitted following his own echo; the village waited for daylight rather than entering the fog. Oren asked whether this was the authentic ending. “It is an ending,” Ilyan replied. Other tellers had made the sailor boast, confess, or remain silent. Fidelity therefore could not mean retrieving a single canonical sequence. It meant recognizing where variation was licensed and where a social action was required. The model's failure was not invention itself. Ilyan had just invented within the tradition. The failure was to sound authoritative while missing the question that made invention answerable to listeners.

The third test began with an access problem. In Mara's catalog, a storm song had been marked restricted to descendants of its singer. A copied transcript had nevertheless entered the laboratory's early training bundle because an old export carried no restriction field. Oren could not prove that the model would reproduce the song, nor could he prove that it had left no trace in learned parameters. Seli's anger was quiet. “You keep asking whether the output is accurate,” she said. “First ask whether the system had a right to learn the source.” The team disconnected the model. Technical uncertainty about memorization did not cancel the clear failure in data handling.

Oren found the export history and isolated every derivative built from the contaminated bundle. Retraining from approved material would take nine days. The laboratory director urged them to continue demonstrations with a filter, but Oren refused: a filter on outputs could not reconstruct consent at ingestion. Mara contacted the singer's descendants through the council rather than assuming that deletion was their only desired remedy. They requested deletion, an audit report, and a rule that future access restrictions travel with every copy. They also asked for an encrypted family deposit outside the model. Their decision separated preservation from availability. Material could be carefully kept without being made public or computationally reusable.

While retraining ran, Mara inventoried the archive's silences. It richly documented elder experts speaking with a familiar linguist. It poorly represented learners correcting one another, mixed Tava and common-language conversation, affectionate teasing among siblings, or arguments in which no participant yielded. Microphones had entered ceremonies more readily than kitchens. Grants had rewarded rare grammatical forms, not the ordinary negotiations through which children discover when a sentence hurts, amuses, or fails. The imbalance did not invalidate the corpus. It constrained its uses. A grammar could describe patterns in the recorded evidence; a model could extend them. Neither could infer an absent social distribution merely by processing the same omissions faster.

The clinic had already placed Ilyan's oxygen unit in the weather station. He tired before noon, and Mara stopped the session despite the calendar. He asked her to leave the recorder off, then described the pressure of being treated as a vault whose door might close. Visitors requested final words, oldest words, pure words. Tava had never remained pure in his mouth; he had borrowed terms, abandoned insults, and altered jokes when their targets changed. What he feared losing was not an untouched object but the ability to be contradicted by another competent speaker. Mara wrote nothing until he later authorized a summary. Consent, she realized again, governed not only whether a file existed but the pace, audience, framing, and future transformations of what a person entrusted.

The retrained model passed the hidden-audio test with slightly lower scores because restricted material had been removed. Oren published both numbers and their incomparable training sets. He also built a source ledger: every generated segment pointed to its approved dataset version, settings, and known exclusions. The ledger could not explain a model's internal associations, but it made administrative lineage inspectable. Seli tested the deletion request with a phrase from the storm song. The system refused retrieval and disclosed only that the relevant source category was unavailable. She nodded without calling the result trust. Compliance in one trial was evidence of one control functioning, not proof that every future use would respect the council's rules.

Seli designed the hardest test: a conversation with herself. She greeted the model in hesitant Tava, revised a case ending midway, and asked it to wait while she searched for a word. The model answered immediately with a complete formal sentence. Its grammar was defensible; its conduct was inept. It neither allowed her repair nor matched the register she had offered. After Oren introduced pauses and shorter alternatives, the exchange became less oppressive but still brittle. Seli deliberately misunderstood a direction. The model repeated the same sentence more slowly instead of reformulating it. “It knows a route through forms,” she said afterward. “It has not learned how two people build a route when one gets lost.”

Mara then corrected Seli's altered case ending. Seli closed her notebook. “Would you have interrupted Ilyan there?” she asked. Mara admitted she would have waited. The asymmetry came from fear: every learner deviation seemed to Mara like one more structure disappearing. Yet constant correction made speech a public examination, narrowing the very domains in which Tava might return. Ilyan distinguished two risks. If no one preserved contrasts, future learners could not recover them. If learners were denied room to change, there might be no future speakers to make any contrast. The archive needed careful description; the community needed conversations whose value did not depend on reproducing an archived norm without variation.

They reviewed Seli's sentence instead of issuing a verdict. In one interpretation, her ending collapsed a distinction between motion toward shore and motion along it. In another, younger islanders were regularizing a pattern already variable in casual recordings. The corpus contained too few peer conversations to decide. Mara proposed marking the form as an error. Seli proposed recording more speakers. Ilyan chose the second proposal and asked Mara to document both analyses. Counterevidence had changed the appropriate conclusion from correction to investigation. This did not make every innovation automatically valuable or every rule oppressive. It meant the team lacked sufficient evidence to convert one expert's expectation into a final account of communal usage.

The council met without the laboratory's cameras. By evening it had drafted conditions for continued work. Source recordings would remain distinct from generated speech. Every synthetic clip would carry an audible common-language introduction outside the clip, so listeners could not mistake it for Ilyan. Training would run only from an approved immutable dataset snapshot; automated allowlist and restriction checks would reject restricted source IDs and every registered derivative before a build could start. The council could suspend deployment, request an audit, and license separate uses for research, teaching, or public exhibition. No model would answer questions about private ritual knowledge. Oren objected that some conditions reduced convenience. Seli replied that convenience for remote users was not the project's sole measure. He removed the objection from the risk register and entered it as a cost.

They also narrowed the model's task. It would no longer present itself as an autonomous Tava speaker. In practice mode, learners could compare their recordings with documented examples, request alternative forms, and see which sources supported a suggestion. When evidence was sparse or speakers disagreed, the system would say so in the common language. It would not fabricate ceremonial narratives. Community teachers could accept, reject, or annotate its proposals, and those judgments would remain separate from the source archive until contributors explicitly approved a new deposit. The design sacrificed seamless conversation. In return, it exposed provenance, disagreement, and abstention—features that a fluent synthetic voice had previously hidden.

The funding contract still concentrated money on computation. Seli opened the budget beside Oren's model diagram. Servers received five years of maintenance; human classes received six months. “Your preserved language will have electricity longer than it has teachers,” she said. This time Oren did not defend the allocation as someone else's department. He calculated what a smaller model and slower release could free. Mara redirected travel funds toward apprenticeships in transcription, teaching, and archive management. The council added paid sessions for fishers, families, and learners, not only Ilyan. Technology remained in the plan, but it ceased to be the plan's temporal center. Transmission required occasions, authority, labor, and people willing to risk imperfect speech together.

At the public demonstration, Oren replaced the laboratory's old claim with four narrower statements. The archive preserved particular recordings and descriptions under specified access rules. The experimental model reproduced some recorded patterns with documented errors. It could support selected learning tasks under community supervision. None of those achievements established that Tava was socially secure. He displayed no single vitality score because speaker number, transmission, domains, materials, attitudes, and institutional conditions did not move together. An archive might improve while household use declined; a class might begin while ceremonial access remained restricted. The audience received a more difficult account, but difficulty here came from preserving distinctions that the former slogan had erased.

Ilyan recorded one final planned session, not a farewell but a boatbuilding lesson for Seli. He showed how to read tension in a bent plank and asked her which verb fit the grain's direction. She chose the form Mara had questioned. Ilyan paused, tested the plank with his thumb, and answered with a different verb. Seli challenged him using an older recording on her tablet. He listened twice and conceded that both forms could work, though he preferred his own. The exchange entered the archive with their consent, competing analyses, and the visible task that gave the words consequence. It offered no pristine endpoint. It documented a language changing through disagreement between two people who intended to continue.

That evening Mara revised the speaker survey. Instead of sorting islanders into fluent and nonfluent boxes, it described comprehension, production, domains, partners, confidence, learning histories, and self-identification. The council retained “last fully fluent speaker” for Ilyan because the phrase expressed a meaningful local judgment, but it prohibited outsiders from treating the category as proof that no other Tava knowledge survived. Seli listed herself as an emerging speaker and archive steward. Naro claimed boatyard fluency and little narrative competence. The aunt declined assessment. Missing data remained missing. A respectful survey could make its criteria visible; it could not compel private knowledge into existence for the convenience of a complete table.

During the last week, Ilyan continued using the oxygen unit that the clinic had already placed in the weather station. Mara shortened every session. Some days he wanted to work; on others he played cards in the common language and refused linguistic significance. Oren learned not to treat unused recording capacity as loss. Seli began a weekly kitchen gathering with three cousins. They cooked, argued, searched the archive, and left long stretches unrecorded. The first new deposit contained a dispute about whether a borrowed word needed a Tava ending. No one resolved it. The metadata named the disagreement, participants, setting, and permissions. For Mara, the unresolved exchange was stronger evidence of use than a flawless sentence generated without stakes.

Ilyan died early in the rain season. The council closed the archive for nine days, as his family requested. The model remained offline. When access resumed, no synthetic voice announced his death or delivered his “final message”; he had authorized neither. At the memorial, Seli spoke Tava, stopped twice, and accepted a word from Naro. Mara understood the first passage and missed part of the second. Later, several listeners offered conflicting translations of one image involving a tide under stone. They preserved the alternatives in the memorial record. The uncertainty did not reduce Ilyan to silence. It showed that meaning had passed into several fallible, answerable relationships rather than one supposedly perfect substitute.

Months later, the practice tool returned under council control. Its first screen contained no human face and made no claim to be Ilyan. Seli recorded a direction to the boat shed. The system proposed two case endings, cited three approved examples, and marked their contexts as unlike hers. She chose a third form and explained why. Mara added the explanation as a learner annotation, not as corrected source data. Oren watched the model decline to rank the answer. Earlier he would have called that a missing feature. Now it was a designed boundary: the system could reveal relevant evidence without manufacturing consensus that the evidence did not contain.

At the next kitchen gathering, Seli set the carved tide bird between two bowls. Her youngest cousin asked in Tava whether it had flown there. Seli answered with the ending for reported knowledge, then caught her own mistake and laughed; everyone in the room had watched her place it. She corrected herself before the model could intervene. Naro proposed an absurd fourth ending, and the table broke into argument. The recorder was present but paused until each person agreed to begin. Outside, the laboratory's server preserved thousands of careful hours. Inside, five imperfect speakers changed one another's sentences in real time. Later, Seli opened a new session file. She titled it only after asking what the speakers wanted it to become.
