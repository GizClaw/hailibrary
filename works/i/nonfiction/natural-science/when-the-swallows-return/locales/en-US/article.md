# When the Swallows Return

Near the spring equinox, Lili opened her orange notebook. She and her younger cousin Yan were staying with Grandma Ruan. Two dark birds swept from the river toward the houses.

Yan raced into the small garden where Grandma Ruan watched the weather. “Their tails look like open scissors!” he cried. Rust-colored throats and long, forked tails marked them as barn swallows.

Yan asked, “Are they last year's birds?” Grandma Ruan lowered her binoculars. “What would tell us?” The birds had no bands or special marks. Lili wrote, “Two arrived, but we don't know if they were here last year.”

The swallows had migrated here to raise chicks. They skimmed over water and rice fields, catching flying insects for food. Yan tried to guess where they would turn next.

A few days later, the two birds circled Grandma Ruan's house. They hovered under the eaves beside a firm wooden beam. “Could they nest there?” Yan asked.

Night rain left soft mud beside the garden path. At sunrise, one swallow picked up mud in its bill. The other carried a dry grass stem.

The two birds pressed small bits of mud against the wall. Working as a pair, they flew back and forth. “They're tiny builders with wings!” Yan shouted.

At noon, Lili saw dry mud and fewer trips. A shower softened the mud, and the birds carried it again. “Maybe soft mud helped,” Lili guessed. “Or did they carry more at sunrise than at noon?” Grandma Ruan asked.

Slowly, a half-cup rose against the wall. The swallows mixed grass into the mud. Under the roof edge, the growing nest stayed dry.

Days later, Lili watched the birds carry grass and small feathers inside. Her notes said, “The swallows had migrated here. They found food over the river, wet mud, and a dry wall under the roof.”

Yan pointed up and at the nest. He guessed, “The spring equinox brought them!” Grandma asked, “Did it bring them, or tell us when?” Lili smiled by her notebook. “They came around that time, but it didn't bring them.”

At dusk, the mud cup clung beneath the eaves. The two swallows rested on a nearby beam. Lili turned to a blank page and wrote, “Watch again tomorrow.”
